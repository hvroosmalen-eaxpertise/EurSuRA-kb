The Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (VSME) is a reporting framework published in 2024 by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG) to enable SMEs to communicate sustainability information on a voluntary basis. It provides a scaled-down, proportionate alternative to the mandatory European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) that apply to large undertakings under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). Although compliance is not legally required for SMEs, sustainability disclosures produced under the VSME are considered relevant for supply-chain transparency, access to capital, and stakeholder engagement.

The VSME is organised into a basic module and an extended module, each comprising thematic chapters that correspond to the major ESRS topic areas, covering climate change and energy, pollution, water use, biodiversity, resource use and circular economy, own workforce, workers in the value chain, affected communities, consumers and end-users, and business conduct. Where the ESRS requires a full Double Materiality Assessment (DMA), the VSME refers to a simplified version of that process, making it more accessible to smaller organisations without dedicated sustainability functions.

The practical application of the VSME was illustrated through the fictionalised SME Bordvol, a tableware and crockery retailer. Using a six-step roadmap, Bordvol identified four material sustainability themes, developed a strategy with three pillars (fair materials, smart choices for lower CO₂ emissions, and good employment practices), conducted baseline measurements, and formulated policies, actions, targets, and KPIs including 100% traceability of sourced goods and climate neutrality by 2035. Communication formats identified as appropriate for SMEs include website publications, organisational statements, and annual sustainability reports.

The CSRD Collectief advises organisations adopting the VSME to establish an internal working group with management approval, study the EFRAG standard directly, apply the six-step roadmap while engaging suppliers and customers where possible, and treat the standard as a shared language for communicating concrete sustainability progress. Beyond regulatory compliance, a structured sustainability strategy is identified as generating value through customer retention, personnel attraction, improved access to capital, and integration of previously fragmented sustainability initiatives.
