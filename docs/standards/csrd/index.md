# Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is the European Union (EU) regulatory instrument adopted in 2023 under which the [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards]] (ESRS) were developed. The CSRD made [[sustainability reporting]] mandatory for large and listed companies operating in the EU from January 2024.[^1] It established the requirement for [[double materiality]] reporting, compelling organisations to disclose both the financial effects of sustainability factors on their business and the impacts of their activities on people and the environment.[^1] The CSRD represents a significant expansion of mandatory sustainability disclosure obligations within the EU regulatory framework, and forms a central element of the EU's broader approach to [[ESG]] regulation.[^2]

The CSRD sits within a broader European regulatory architecture that includes the European Green Deal (2021) and the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR, 2021), and is itself part of a longer policy trajectory initiated by the Paris Agreement (2015) and the United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs, 2015).[^3]

## Scope and Predecessor

The CSRD significantly expands the scope of mandatory sustainability reporting compared to its predecessor, the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD).[^2] It requires qualifying companies to disclose information on environmental and social impacts and governance practices, reflecting a broader shift from voluntary [[corporate social responsibility]] commitments toward regulated, enforceable obligations.[^2]

Although the CSRD does not directly obligate small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), it creates significant downstream information demands on them. Large CSRD-obligated companies require sustainability data from their supply chains, meaning SMEs acting as raw-material suppliers, semi-finished-goods producers, manufacturers, or distributors face indirect pressure to disclose sustainability information.[^3] This demand was a primary driver behind the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group's (EFRAG's) development of the [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs]] (VSME) framework in 2024.[^3] The case study of Bordvol, a fictionalised SME, illustrates this dynamic: Bordvol had commenced CSRD preparation before its direct obligations were revised, subsequently evaluating whether to continue voluntary reporting under the VSME.[^3]

## Extraterritorial Effects

Although the CSRD applies directly to companies operating within the EU, its influence extends beyond EU borders. Businesses in countries such as Bosnia and Herzegovina, despite operating outside the EU, face indirect pressure from CSRD requirements through supply chain relationships with EU-based partners.[^2]
