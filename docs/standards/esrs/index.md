# European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)

The European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) were developed under the European Union's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and published in 2023, becoming mandatory for large and listed companies operating in the EU from January 2024.[^1] ESRS covers the full [[ESG]] scope, encompassing social, governance, and climate themes. A defining feature of ESRS is the requirement for [[double materiality]] reporting, under which companies must disclose both [[financial materiality]]—the effect of sustainability factors on the organisation's financial position—and [[impact materiality]]—the organisation's effects on people and the environment.[^1] ESRS applies specifically to organisations subject to EU regulatory requirements under the CSRD.

## Thematic Coverage

The ESRS addresses a broad range of sustainability topics, including:[^2]

- Climate change and energy
- Pollution
- Water use and marine resources
- Biodiversity and ecosystems
- Resource use and [[circular economy]]
- Own workforce
- Workers in the value chain
- Affected communities
- Consumers and end-users
- Business conduct

## Double Materiality Assessment

Reporting entities subject to the ESRS are required to conduct a full [[Double Materiality Assessment]] (DMA), which evaluates both the financial effects of sustainability matters on the enterprise and the enterprise's effects on society and the environment.[^2] This dual lens distinguishes the ESRS framework from single-materiality standards and is central to its design logic.

## Relationship to the VSME Standard

The [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs]] (VSME) was developed as a proportionate counterpart to the ESRS, with its basic and extended module chapters mapped directly to corresponding ESRS topic areas.[^2] For example, ESRS chapter E1 on climate change and energy corresponds to VSME chapters B2, C2, B3, C3, and C4, while ESRS chapter S1 on own workforce maps to VSME chapters B2, C2, B8, B9, B10, C5, C6, C7, and C9.[^2] This structural alignment ensures that [[small and medium-sized enterprises]] (SMEs) reporting under the VSME produce information that is intelligible and useful to large CSRD-obligated undertakings in their value chains.[^2]
