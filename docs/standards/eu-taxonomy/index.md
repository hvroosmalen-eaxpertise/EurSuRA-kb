# EU Taxonomy

The EU Taxonomy, established by Regulation (EU) 2020/852 (the Taxonomy Regulation), is the European Union's common classification system for environmentally sustainable economic activities. It is the measurement layer under the EU sustainable-finance *acquis*: where the [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)]] mandates disclosure, the [[Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR)]] regulates how financial products talk about sustainability, and the [[Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)]] regulates conduct, the Taxonomy defines what actually counts as "environmentally sustainable" in euros, revenues, capex and opex.

The Taxonomy entered into force on 12 July 2020. Its technical screening criteria are set out in delegated acts: the Climate Delegated Act (Regulation (EU) 2021/2139) covers climate change mitigation and adaptation; the Environmental Delegated Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2486) covers the remaining four objectives. Disclosure obligations under Article 8 apply in phases from 2022 (climate objectives) and 2024 (remaining objectives).

## The Six Environmental Objectives

An economic activity qualifies as environmentally sustainable only if it makes a substantial contribution to at least one of six environmental objectives:

1. **Climate change mitigation** — avoiding or reducing [[Greenhouse Gas Protocol]], or enhancing greenhouse-gas removals.
2. **Climate change adaptation** — reducing or preventing the adverse impacts of the current and expected future climate on people, nature or assets.
3. **Sustainable use and protection of water and marine resources.**
4. **Transition to a [[circular economy]]** — including waste prevention, re-use and recycling.
5. **Pollution prevention and control.**
6. **Protection and restoration of biodiversity and ecosystems.**

Each objective is populated with a growing catalogue of economic activities (NACE-code based) and, for each activity, quantitative technical screening criteria.

## The Four Taxonomy Tests

To be reported as Taxonomy-aligned, an activity must pass four cumulative tests:

1. **Substantial contribution** to at least one of the six objectives, as specified by the applicable technical screening criterion. Example: an electricity-generation activity contributes substantially to climate mitigation when its life-cycle emissions fall below 100 g CO₂e/kWh.
2. **Do No Significant Harm (DNSH)** to any of the other five objectives. DNSH criteria sit alongside substantial-contribution criteria for every activity, and together they define the activity-specific technical screening criteria.
3. **Minimum safeguards.** Compliance with the [[OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises]], the [[UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights]], the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work, and the International Bill of Human Rights. In practice this is a company-level test, not an activity-level one.
4. **Compliance with the technical screening criteria** themselves — the detailed numeric thresholds and qualitative requirements in the delegated acts.

An activity that is included in the delegated acts (*eligible*) but fails one or more tests is reported as *not aligned*. An activity that is not included at all is *not eligible*.

## Transitional and Enabling Activities

Two categories relax the model to recognise activities that help the transition without being inherently low-impact today:

- **Transitional activities** (climate mitigation only). Activities for which there is no technologically or economically feasible low-carbon alternative, which support the transition to a climate-neutral economy, and which do not lock in carbon-intensive assets. The technical screening criteria are tightened over time (for example, electricity generation from natural gas under strict GHG caps).
- **Enabling activities**. Activities that directly enable others to make a substantial contribution — the classic example is the manufacture of wind turbines or heat-pump components.

Both categories count as substantially contributing when their criteria are met.

## Article 8 — The Disclosure Regime

Article 8 of the Taxonomy Regulation obliges large non-financial undertakings (currently those in scope of NFRD / CSRD) to disclose, in their management report:

- the proportion of turnover from Taxonomy-aligned activities;
- the proportion of capital expenditure (CapEx) linked to Taxonomy-aligned activities, including CapEx plans toward alignment; and
- the proportion of operating expenditure (OpEx) linked to Taxonomy-aligned activities.

Financial undertakings (credit institutions, asset managers, investment firms, insurers, reinsurers) disclose equivalent KPIs reflecting their lending, investment, underwriting and asset-management exposures — notably the Green Asset Ratio (GAR) for banks.

Disclosures are made in standardised templates set out in the Taxonomy Article 8 Disclosures Delegated Act (Regulation (EU) 2021/2178), reported alongside the sustainability statement under [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)]] E1.

## Interactions

- **CSRD / ESRS.** ESRS E1 (climate) requires Taxonomy KPIs for in-scope undertakings; the two regimes share definitions of GHG emissions and climate transition plans.
- **SFDR.** Financial products that make environmental claims rely on the Taxonomy for the "sustainable investment" and "do no significant harm" calculations in their product disclosures.
- **EU Green Bond Standard** (Regulation (EU) 2023/2631). The "European green bond" label is reserved for bonds whose use of proceeds funds Taxonomy-aligned activities, with limited transition flexibility.
- **CSDDD.** No direct link — CSDDD regulates conduct — but Taxonomy minimum safeguards share the same OECD / UNGP underpinning as the CSDDD due-diligence process.

## 2024–2026 Developments and the *Omnibus* Simplification

The 2024 "Taxonomy Omnibus" and the 2025 *Omnibus I* simplification package together introduced:

- the Environmental Delegated Act (operational from 2024) populating the remaining four objectives;
- amendments to the Climate Delegated Act expanding transitional activities and refining DNSH;
- a materiality threshold allowing in-scope undertakings to omit activities below 10 % of each KPI from detailed reporting;
- simplified reporting templates with fewer mandatory datapoints; and
- a staged roll-out of alignment reporting for undertakings newly in scope of CSRD after the thresholds were raised.

The practical effect is a Taxonomy that remains ambitious in substance but less onerous in reporting form.

## Implications for SMEs

[[SMEs]] are not directly in Article 8 scope. The Taxonomy nonetheless reaches them through two channels:

- **Supply-chain data demands.** CSRD-obligated buyers and SFDR-reporting investors increasingly request activity-level data to compute their own alignment ratios; SMEs with revenue tied to clearly Taxonomy-eligible activities (energy renovation, renewable installation, circular-materials recovery, electric mobility) have both an obligation to answer and an opportunity to position themselves.
- **Access to green capital.** Taxonomy-aligned CapEx attracts green-labelled lending and green-bond financing — including the EU Green Bond Standard — on terms often more favourable than conventional finance.

The [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs (VSME)]] Comprehensive module includes datapoints — share of revenue, CapEx and OpEx by activity, GHG intensity — specifically designed to flow upward into a buyer's Taxonomy calculation.
