# Double Materiality Across Regimes

Double materiality is a core principle in sustainability reporting, requiring organisations to disclose both the financial effects of sustainability factors on their business (financial materiality) and the impacts of their activities on people and the environment (impact materiality). This concept is central to the **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)** and the **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)**, which mandate double materiality reporting for large and listed companies in the EU. In contrast, the **Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)**, while not mandating double materiality, encourages organisations to disclose both financial and impact materiality as part of a broader sustainability disclosure framework. This article explores how double materiality is defined and operationalised across these regimes.

| **Aspect**               | **CSRD/ESRS**                                                                 | **GRI**                                                                 |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**           | Requires disclosure of both financial and impact materiality.                 | Encourages disclosure of both financial and impact materiality.         |
| **Mandatory**            | Yes (for large and listed companies in the EU).                              | No (voluntary for all organisations).                                  |
| **Focus**                | Financial and impact materiality as central to sustainability reporting.    | Broad sustainability disclosure, including financial and impact aspects. |
| **Operationalisation**   | Through the Double Materiality Assessment (DMA) and ESRS standards.         | Through GRI Standards, which include indicators for both materialities.  |
| **Regulatory Context**   | Part of the EU's broader ESG regulatory framework.                           | A global, voluntary framework used across sectors and geographies.       |

The CSRD and ESRS mandate double materiality as a regulatory requirement, with the ESRS providing detailed guidance on how to assess and report both financial and impact materiality. This includes a structured approach to identifying and disclosing sustainability matters that are material to the organisation's financial position and its effects on society and the environment. The CSRD also extends its influence beyond the EU through supply chain requirements, indirectly pressuring SMEs to disclose sustainability information.

In contrast, the GRI does not mandate double materiality but encourages organisations to disclose both aspects as part of a comprehensive sustainability report. The GRI Standards 2021 edition provides guidance on how to identify and report material topics across economic, environmental, and social themes, with a focus on stakeholder engagement and accountability. While the GRI does not require a formal double materiality assessment, it aligns with the principles of double materiality by emphasizing the importance of both financial and impact considerations in sustainability reporting.

Both frameworks share a common goal of enhancing transparency and accountability in sustainability reporting, but they differ in their regulatory approach and operationalisation. The CSRD/ESRS framework is more prescriptive and regulatory, while the GRI offers a flexible, voluntary approach that can be adapted to various organisational contexts.
