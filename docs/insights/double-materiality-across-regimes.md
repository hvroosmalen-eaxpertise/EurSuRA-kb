# Double Materiality Across Regimes

Double materiality is a core principle in sustainability reporting, requiring organisations to disclose both the financial effects of sustainability factors on their business (financial materiality) and the impacts of their activities on people and the environment (impact materiality). The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) mandate double materiality reporting for large and listed companies in the EU, while the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) provides a voluntary framework that also incorporates elements of double materiality, albeit with a broader focus on stakeholder engagement and impact disclosure.

| **Aspect**               | **CSRD/ESRS**                                                                 | **GRI**                                                                 |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Definition**           | Requires disclosure of both financial and impact materiality.                 | Encourages disclosure of both financial and impact materiality, but not as strictly mandated. |
| **Mandatory vs. Voluntary** | Mandatory for large and listed companies in the EU.                           | Voluntary for all organisations globally.                               |
| **Focus**                | Financial and impact materiality, aligned with EU regulatory goals.           | Broad stakeholder engagement, with a focus on impact disclosure.         |
| **Structure**            | Structured around specific sustainability topics and double materiality assessment. | Organised around economic, environmental, and social themes.             |
| **Implementation**       | Requires a full Double Materiality Assessment (DMA).                          | Encourages a similar approach but is not explicitly mandated.            |
| **Relationship to SMEs** | Indirectly affects SMEs through supply chain demands.                        | Directly applicable to SMEs, with a simplified framework.                |

The CSRD and ESRS are part of the EU's regulatory framework, which includes the European Green Deal and the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR), reflecting a policy trajectory initiated by the Paris Agreement and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). In contrast, the GRI is a voluntary framework that is widely adopted globally and is considered most appropriate for organisations seeking broad impact disclosure and multi-stakeholder accountability.

The CSRD and ESRS require organisations to conduct a full Double Materiality Assessment (DMA), which evaluates both the financial effects of sustainability matters on the enterprise and the enterprise's effects on society and the environment. This dual lens distinguishes the ESRS framework from single-materiality standards and is central to its design logic. While the GRI does not explicitly mandate a DMA, it encourages organisations to consider both financial and impact materiality in their reporting, aligning with the principles of double materiality.

The Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs (VSME) was developed as a proportionate counterpart to the ESRS, with its basic and extended module chapters mapped directly to corresponding ESRS topic areas. This structural alignment ensures that SMEs reporting under the VSME produce information that is intelligible and useful to large CSRD-obligated undertakings in their value chains. The GRI, while not specifically tailored for SMEs, provides a flexible framework that can be adapted to the needs of smaller organisations.
