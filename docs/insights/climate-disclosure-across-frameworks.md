# Climate Disclosure Across Frameworks

An organisation reporting on climate typically engages four regimes at once: the
[[ESRS]] (mandatory under the [[CSRD]]), the [[TCFD]] recommendations, the
[[GHG Protocol]] for emissions accounting, and the [[EU Taxonomy]] for classifying
activities. They overlap heavily in substance while differing in legal status and
emphasis. This page relates them around the practical question an SME faces: *what
do I actually measure and disclose?*

## How the regimes relate

| Regime | Status | Climate focus | Emissions basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[ESRS]] E1 | Mandatory (CSRD) | Transition plan, targets, physical & transition risk | [[Scope 1 Emissions]], [[Scope 2 Emissions]], [[Scope 3 Emissions]] |
| [[TCFD]] | Voluntary / superseded by ISSB | Governance, strategy, risk, metrics & targets | References GHG Protocol |
| [[GHG Protocol]] | Accounting standard | Quantifying the inventory | Defines Scope 1/2/3 |
| [[EU Taxonomy]] | Mandatory classification | Whether an activity is environmentally sustainable | Underpins alignment metrics |

## The common spine: greenhouse gas accounting

All four regimes ultimately rest on the same measurement layer. The
[[GHG Protocol]] defines [[Scope 1 Emissions]], [[Scope 2 Emissions]], and
[[Scope 3 Emissions]]; ESRS E1 and the climate metrics expected under the
[[TCFD]] structure both consume those figures rather than redefining them.

## Materiality and scope

Where the regimes diverge most is *why* a disclosure is required. ESRS applies
[[Double Materiality]], capturing both impact and financial materiality, whereas
the TCFD/ISSB lineage is primarily financially oriented. The [[EU Taxonomy]]
adds a third lens — whether an activity meets technical screening criteria.
