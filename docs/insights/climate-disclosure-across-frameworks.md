# Climate Disclosure Across Frameworks

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operating in or supplying to the EU face an interlocking set of climate disclosure expectations drawn from at least four major frameworks: the [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards]] (ESRS), the Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol, and the EU Taxonomy Regulation. Although each framework has a distinct origin and audience, they share substantial common ground—particularly around GHG emissions measurement, climate risk, and the classification of sustainable activities—allowing a well-structured disclosure to serve multiple regimes simultaneously.

## Overview of the Four Frameworks

| Framework | Origin | Primary Audience | Mandatory or Voluntary (EU SME)? | Core Climate Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESRS | EU / EFRAG, 2023 | Large and listed EU companies; SME value-chain partners | Mandatory for large companies; voluntary VSME available for SMEs | [[Double materiality]]: financial + impact | 
| TCFD | FSB Task Force, 2017 | Listed companies globally | Mandatory in UK, Japan, NZ; voluntary elsewhere | Single materiality: climate financial risk |
| GHG Protocol | WRI / WBCSD | All organisations | Voluntary; underlies most mandatory regimes | Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions measurement |
| EU Taxonomy | EU Regulation | Financial market participants; reporting companies | Mandatory for large companies; reference for SMEs | Classification of environmentally sustainable activities |

## The ESRS and the SME Entry Point

The [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards]] were published in 2023 and became mandatory for large and listed EU companies from January 2024 [[esrs]]. They require a full [[Double Materiality Assessment]] (DMA), under which companies must report both [[financial materiality]]—the effect of sustainability factors on the organisation's financial position—and [[impact materiality]]—the organisation's effects on people and the environment [[esrs]].

Although SMEs are not directly subject to the CSRD, they are indirectly drawn in because their large-company customers and supply-chain partners are obligated to report on value-chain sustainability data [[esrs]]. To address this, EFRAG developed the [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs]] (VSME), whose basic and extended modules map directly to ESRS topic areas [[esrs]]. For example, ESRS chapter E1 on climate change and energy corresponds to VSME chapters B2, C2, B3, C3, and C4 [[esrs]]. This structural alignment means that an SME disclosing under the VSME produces information that is immediately legible to its CSRD-obligated value-chain partners [[esrs]].

## TCFD: The International Risk-Disclosure Baseline

The TCFD was launched in 2017 to help organisations disclose climate-related financial risks in a consistent and decision-useful manner [[tcfd]]. Its framework is structured around four core pillars:

1. **Governance** – Board and management oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities
2. **Strategy** – Actual and potential impacts of climate risks and opportunities on business, strategy, and financial planning
3. **Risk Management** – How climate-related risks are identified, assessed, and managed
4. **Metrics and Targets** – Metrics used to assess and manage climate-related risks and opportunities

TCFD disclosures are mandatory for listed companies in the United Kingdom, Japan, and New Zealand [[tcfd]]. Crucially, the TCFD subsequently served as the foundational basis for standards developed by the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB), and its recommendations also underpin the methodology used by the Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) [[tcfd]].

For an SME that exports to or is listed in TCFD-mandatory jurisdictions, or that reports to CDP at a customer's request, the TCFD's four pillars provide the conceptual scaffold. The ESRS likewise structures climate disclosure around governance, strategy, impacts/risks/opportunities, and metrics—demonstrating direct architectural overlap with TCFD [[esrs]] [[tcfd]].

## GHG Protocol: The Measurement Foundation

The GHG Protocol underpins the emissions measurement methodology used across all the above frameworks. Business environmental strategies typically include targets to reduce GHG emissions, invest in renewable energy, and promote sustainable resource use [[ghg-protocol]]. The three emission scopes are:

- **Scope 1**: Direct emissions from owned or controlled sources
- **Scope 2**: Indirect emissions from purchased energy
- **Scope 3**: All other indirect emissions in the value chain

The inclusion of Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions within the VSME framework reflects alignment with the ESRS topic of climate change and energy [[ghg-protocol]]. Scope 3 emissions are particularly relevant for SMEs embedded in supply chains, as upstream and downstream value-chain partners generate emissions data that contribute to the reported impacts of larger CSRD-obligated entities [[ghg-protocol]]. The Bordvol case study illustrates this in practice: the organisation's sustainability dashboard incorporated total CO₂ emissions across all three scopes as measurable key performance indicators (KPIs), with a target of climate neutrality by 2035, and CO₂ data was sourced via a logistics service provider with energy consumption data gathered per production site [[ghg-protocol]].

### How the Scopes Map Across Frameworks

| Emission Scope | ESRS / VSME | TCFD | GHG Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope 1 (direct) | Required under E1/VSME C2-C3 | Reported as metric under Metrics & Targets | Core measurement category |
| Scope 2 (purchased energy) | Required under E1/VSME C2-C3 | Reported as metric under Metrics & Targets | Core measurement category |
| Scope 3 (value chain) | Required; especially relevant for SME supply-chain data | Increasingly expected under Metrics & Targets | Core measurement category; most complex |

## EU Taxonomy: Classifying What Is Sustainable

The EU Taxonomy Regulation establishes a classification system for environmentally sustainable economic activities and forms a key component of the EU sustainable finance architecture [[eu-taxonomy]]. It operates alongside the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR), which requires financial market participants to disclose how sustainability risks are integrated into investment decisions and the adverse sustainability impacts of financial products [[eu-taxonomy]].

Together, these instruments direct capital toward sustainable outcomes [[eu-taxonomy]]. For an SME, the EU Taxonomy is listed among the primary reference frameworks that businesses may need to consider when structuring ESG strategies and reporting [[eu-taxonomy]]. Where a company's activities qualify as taxonomy-aligned, this can be disclosed within the ESRS framework—particularly under the financial materiality dimension of the DMA—and may influence how financial institutions assess the SME's sustainability credentials [[eu-taxonomy]] [[esrs]].

The EU Taxonomy also intersects with the TCFD's Strategy pillar: demonstrating that business activities are taxonomy-aligned can be part of disclosing how climate-related opportunities are being pursued and how the business strategy is resilient to a low-carbon transition [[tcfd]] [[eu-taxonomy]].

## Where the Frameworks Overlap

Despite their different origins and mandatory scopes, the four frameworks converge on several disclosure elements that an SME can address once to satisfy multiple regimes:

| Disclosure Element | ESRS / VSME | TCFD | GHG Protocol | EU Taxonomy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GHG emissions (Scopes 1, 2, 3) | ✓ (E1 / VSME C2-C3) | ✓ (Metrics & Targets) | ✓ (foundational) | Contextual (activity eligibility) |
| Climate governance | ✓ | ✓ (Governance pillar) | — | — |
| Climate risk and strategy | ✓ (DMA, impact + financial) | ✓ (Strategy, Risk Management pillars) | — | Aligned-activity classification |
| Targets and KPIs | ✓ (VSME includes KPIs) | ✓ (Metrics & Targets) | ✓ (target-setting guidance) | — |
| Taxonomy alignment | ✓ (ESRS references EU Taxonomy) | Implicit (transition opportunity) | — | ✓ (core purpose) |
| Double materiality | ✓ (mandatory under ESRS) | ✗ (single materiality only) | — | — |

A notable divergence is **materiality approach**: the ESRS mandates [[double materiality]], assessing both the financial effects on the company and the company's effects on the world [[esrs]], whereas the TCFD is explicitly oriented toward single (financial) materiality—the effect of climate on the organisation [[tcfd]]. An SME preparing a VSME-aligned disclosure will therefore go further than a pure TCFD disclosure requires, but the additional effort covers the impact dimension that the ESRS's value-chain logic demands.

## A Practical Disclosure Pathway for an SME

Drawing the frameworks together, an SME can construct a climate disclosure in the following sequence:

1. **Measure emissions** using the GHG Protocol's Scope 1, 2, and 3 categories [[ghg-protocol]]. The Bordvol approach—sourcing CO₂ data from logistics providers and gathering energy data per site—illustrates a proportionate method [[ghg-protocol]].
2. **Structure the narrative** around the TCFD's four pillars (Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, Metrics and Targets), which are architecturally mirrored in the ESRS climate standard and therefore serve both frameworks simultaneously [[tcfd]] [[esrs]].
3. **Report under the VSME**, using the basic and extended modules that map to ESRS E1 (climate change and energy) and related chapters [[esrs]]. This ensures the output is usable by CSRD-obligated customers and partners [[esrs]].
4. **Reference EU Taxonomy alignment** where applicable, identifying which business activities meet the classification criteria for environmentally sustainable economic activities [[eu-taxonomy]]. This supports both the financial materiality dimension of the DMA and investor-facing disclosures under SFDR [[eu-taxonomy]] [[esrs]].
5. **Set a climate target**, such as a climate neutrality date, and express it as a measurable KPI—as illustrated by the Bordvol case study [[ghg-protocol]]—to satisfy the Metrics and Targets pillar of TCFD and the corresponding VSME module.
