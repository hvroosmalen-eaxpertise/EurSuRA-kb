# The SME Reporting Pathway

When small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operate within the European Union, they may choose between voluntary sustainability reporting under the Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for SMEs (VSME) and mandatory reporting under the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), which apply to large undertakings under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). The VSME provides a scaled-down, proportionate alternative to the ESRS, enabling SMEs to communicate sustainability information on a voluntary basis, while the ESRS mandates comprehensive sustainability disclosures for large companies. The CSRD, which underpins the ESRS, does not directly apply to SMEs but creates indirect reporting demands through supply chain transparency requirements. This article explores the conditions under which SMEs might apply the VSME versus the ESRS, and how these regimes interact.

## When VSME Applies Versus Full ESRS/CSRD

The VSME is designed for SMEs that wish to report sustainability information voluntarily, without legal obligation. It offers a simplified version of the double materiality assessment required by the ESRS, making it more accessible for smaller organisations without dedicated sustainability functions. In contrast, the ESRS and CSRD apply to large companies and require full double materiality assessments, covering a broad range of sustainability topics. The CSRD, while not directly applicable to SMEs, imposes indirect reporting obligations through its supply chain transparency requirements, which have driven the development of the VSME framework.

| **Aspect** | **VSME** | **ESRS/CSRD** |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| **Applicability** | Voluntary for SMEs | Mandatory for large undertakings |
| **Reporting Scope** | Thematic chapters aligned with ESRS topics | Full coverage of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) themes |
| **Materiality Assessment** | Simplified version of double materiality | Full double materiality assessment |
| **Regulatory Obligation** | No legal requirement | Legal requirement under CSRD |
| **Purpose** | Enhance transparency and stakeholder engagement | Ensure compliance with EU sustainability regulations |

## How Voluntary and Mandatory Regimes Relate

The VSME and ESRS/CSRD are structured to complement one another, with the VSME serving as a proportionate counterpart to the ESRS. The thematic coverage of the VSME is mapped directly to the ESRS, ensuring that information reported under the VSME is intelligible and useful to large companies under the CSRD. This alignment facilitates supply chain transparency and supports the broader goal of integrated sustainability reporting across the value chain.

The CSRD, which mandates the ESRS, creates indirect reporting demands on SMEs through its supply chain transparency requirements. Large companies under the CSRD require sustainability data from their suppliers, which has led to the development of the VSME as a voluntary reporting framework for SMEs. This dynamic is illustrated by the case study of Bordvol, a fictionalised SME that began preparing for CSRD obligations before its direct obligations were revised, subsequently evaluating whether to continue voluntary reporting under the VSME.

## Value of Structured Sustainability Strategy

Beyond regulatory compliance, a structured sustainability strategy is identified as generating value through customer retention, personnel attraction, improved access to capital, and integration of previously fragmented sustainability initiatives. The CSRD Collectief advises organisations adopting the VSME to establish an internal working group with management approval, study the EFRAG standard directly, apply the six-step roadmap while engaging suppliers and customers where possible, and treat the standard as a shared language for communicating concrete sustainability progress.
