# The SME Reporting Pathway: When VSME Applies Versus Full ESRS/CSRD

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operating in or alongside the EU market face a dual landscape: the mandatory [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)]] (CSRD) regime, which directly obliges large and listed companies, and the voluntary [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs]] (VSME) framework, which offers SMEs a proportionate, structured alternative. Although SMEs are not directly compelled by the CSRD, they face significant indirect pressure from it through supply-chain relationships, making the choice of reporting pathway a practical strategic decision rather than a purely academic one. Understanding when each regime applies, how they relate structurally, and what the transition between them looks like is essential for any SME navigating sustainability disclosure.

---

## Who the Regimes Are Designed For

The two frameworks serve distinct legal categories of organisation, but their boundaries touch at the supply chain.

| Dimension | CSRD / ESRS | VSME |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal status** | Mandatory | Voluntary |
| **Primary addressees** | Large and listed EU companies | SMEs |
| **Publication year** | 2023 (ESRS published; mandatory from Jan 2024) | 2024 |
| **Developer** | EFRAG under CSRD mandate | EFRAG |
| **Materiality process** | Full [[Double Materiality Assessment]] (DMA) | Simplified materiality process |
| **Compliance enforced?** | Yes, for qualifying companies | No |

The [[CSRD]] made sustainability reporting mandatory for large and listed companies operating in the EU from January 2024 [[csrd]]. The [[ESRS]] were the technical standards developed under that directive, covering the full Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) scope [[esrs]]. The VSME, published in 2024 by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG), was explicitly designed as a "scaled-down, proportionate alternative" for organisations that do not fall within the CSRD's direct scope [[vsme]].

## The Indirect Pressure on SMEs: Why Voluntary Is Not Irrelevant

Although the CSRD does not directly obligate SMEs, it creates substantial downstream information demands. Large CSRD-obligated companies require sustainability data from their supply chains, meaning SMEs acting as raw-material suppliers, semi-finished-goods producers, manufacturers, or distributors face indirect pressure to disclose sustainability information [[csrd]]. This supply-chain dynamic was a primary driver behind EFRAG's development of the VSME framework [[csrd]].

The fictionalised SME case study Bordvol illustrates this dynamic concretely: Bordvol, a tableware and crockery retailer, had begun CSRD preparation before its direct obligations were revised, and subsequently evaluated whether to continue voluntary reporting under the VSME [[csrd]]. This reflects a real-world pattern in which SMEs initially engage with sustainability reporting in response to customer or investor expectations, then seek a framework calibrated to their actual capacity.

Beyond regulatory compliance, the VSME sources identify additional value drivers that make voluntary reporting strategically rational for SMEs: customer retention, personnel attraction, improved access to capital, and integration of previously fragmented sustainability initiatives [[vsme]].

## Structural Alignment: How the VSME Maps to the ESRS

A critical design feature of the VSME is that its topic chapters are mapped directly to corresponding ESRS topic areas [[esrs]]. Both frameworks cover the same thematic terrain:

- Climate change and energy
- Pollution
- Water use
- Biodiversity and ecosystems
- Resource use and circular economy
- Own workforce
- Workers in the value chain
- Affected communities
- Consumers and end-users
- Business conduct

[[esrs]] [[vsme]]

This structural alignment is not incidental. It ensures that SMEs reporting under the VSME produce information that is intelligible and useful to large CSRD-obligated undertakings in their value chains [[esrs]]. Specific cross-walk examples illustrate the mapping: ESRS chapter E1 on climate change and energy corresponds to VSME chapters B2, C2, B3, C3, and C4, while ESRS chapter S1 on own workforce maps to VSME chapters B2, C2, B8, B9, B10, C5, C6, C7, and C9 [[esrs]]. This means an SME reporting under the VSME is, in effect, speaking the same language as the large companies whose supply-chain data requests it receives.

## The Materiality Threshold: Full DMA Versus Simplified Process

One of the most significant practical differences between the two regimes is the materiality assessment requirement. Under the ESRS, reporting entities must conduct a full [[Double Materiality Assessment]] (DMA), which evaluates both the financial effects of sustainability matters on the enterprise ([[financial materiality]]) and the enterprise's effects on society and the environment ([[impact materiality]]) [[esrs]]. This dual lens is central to the ESRS design logic and distinguishes it from single-materiality standards [[esrs]].

The VSME replaces this with a simplified version of the double materiality process, making it accessible to smaller organisations without dedicated sustainability functions [[vsme]]. The Bordvol case study demonstrates this in practice: using a six-step roadmap, Bordvol identified four material sustainability themes, developed a strategy with three pillars (fair materials, smart choices for lower CO₂ emissions, and good employment practices), and formulated policies, actions, targets, and key performance indicators (KPIs) including 100% traceability of sourced goods and climate neutrality by 2035 [[vsme]].

## The VSME's Internal Structure: Basic and Extended Modules

The VSME is organised into a basic module and an extended module, both comprising thematic chapters corresponding to major ESRS topic areas [[vsme]]. This tiered structure allows SMEs to begin with a lighter disclosure footprint and expand over time, or to calibrate their reporting depth to the specific information demands of their value-chain partners. The basic module provides a foundation; the extended module allows greater granularity where stakeholder expectations or sector-specific considerations require it [[vsme]].

This modularity also creates a practical migration pathway: an SME that outgrows the VSME—whether because it crosses the size threshold for CSRD direct applicability, or because its customers and investors demand ESRS-level disclosure—has already built reporting practices aligned with ESRS topic areas and can scale accordingly.

## The Broader Regulatory Architecture

The CSRD does not sit in isolation. It is embedded within a broader European regulatory architecture that includes the European Green Deal (2021) and the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR, 2021), and traces a longer policy trajectory initiated by the Paris Agreement (2015) and the United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs, 2015) [[csrd]]. The CSRD also represents a significant expansion beyond its predecessor, the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD), reflecting a broader shift from voluntary corporate social responsibility (CSR) commitments toward regulated, enforceable obligations [[csrd]].

For SMEs, this trajectory matters because it signals the direction of travel. What is voluntary today under the VSME reflects the substantive logic of what is mandatory under the CSRD. SMEs that build VSME-aligned reporting practices are therefore positioning themselves within a regulatory direction that is likely to tighten over time, particularly as the extraterritorial effects of the CSRD reach SMEs in non-EU countries through their supply-chain relationships with EU-based partners [[csrd]].

## Practical Guidance for SMEs Adopting the VSME

The CSRD Collectief advises organisations adopting the VSME to:

1. Establish an internal working group with management approval
2. Study the EFRAG standard directly
3. Apply the six-step roadmap while engaging suppliers and customers where possible
4. Treat the standard as a shared language for communicating concrete sustainability progress

[[vsme]]

Appropriate communication formats identified for SMEs include website publications, organisational statements, and annual sustainability reports [[vsme]].

## Summary: When Each Regime Applies

| Situation | Applicable Regime |
|---|---|
| Large or listed EU company (from Jan 2024) | CSRD / ESRS — mandatory |
| SME with no direct CSRD obligation, but supply-chain pressure | VSME — voluntary but strategically advisable |
| SME beginning sustainability reporting for the first time | VSME basic module |
| SME with more advanced reporting capacity or granular stakeholder demands | VSME extended module |
| SME crossing CSRD size thresholds over time | Transition from VSME to ESRS (structural alignment facilitates this) |
| Non-EU SME supplying EU-based large companies | VSME — voluntary, but indirectly pressured by CSRD extraterritorial effects |
