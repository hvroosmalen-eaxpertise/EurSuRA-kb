# The SME Reporting Pathway

When a small or medium enterprise (SME) operates within the European Union, it may choose between voluntary sustainability reporting under the Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (VSME) and mandatory reporting under the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), which apply to large undertakings under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). The VSME provides a scaled-down, proportionate alternative to the ESRS, enabling SMEs to communicate sustainability information on a voluntary basis, while the ESRS and CSRD impose mandatory reporting obligations on larger entities. The relationship between these regimes is shaped by the regulatory context, the scope of sustainability topics covered, and the depth of materiality assessments required.

## Voluntary vs. Mandatory Reporting Regimes

The VSME is designed for SMEs and allows them to report on sustainability issues without legal obligation, whereas the ESRS and CSRD require large companies to disclose comprehensive sustainability information. The VSME includes a basic module and an extended module, each covering thematic chapters aligned with the ESRS, but with simplified requirements. For instance, while the ESRS mandates a full Double Materiality Assessment (DMA), the VSME refers to a simplified version of this process, making it more accessible for smaller organizations [[vsme]].

| **Aspect**               | **VSME (Voluntary)**                          | **ESRS/CSRD (Mandatory)**                     |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **Applicability**        | SMEs (no legal obligation)                    | Large undertakings and listed companies       |
| **Scope of Reporting**   | Thematic chapters aligned with ESRS topics    | Full coverage of ESG topics with double materiality |
| **Materiality Assessment** | Simplified version of DMA                    | Full DMA (financial and impact materiality)   |
| **Purpose**              | Enhance transparency, access to capital       | Regulatory compliance, stakeholder engagement |
| **Implementation**       | Six-step roadmap, internal working group     | Formal reporting process, compliance with EU law |

The CSRD, which mandates the ESRS, creates indirect reporting pressures on SMEs through supply chain relationships. Large companies under the CSRD require sustainability data from their suppliers, prompting SMEs to consider voluntary reporting under the VSME [[csrd]]. This dynamic is illustrated by the fictional SME Bordvol, which began preparing for CSRD obligations before its direct responsibilities were revised, and evaluated whether to continue reporting under the VSME [[vsme]].

## Overlap and Integration Between Regimes

The VSME and ESRS are structurally aligned, with the VSME's thematic chapters mapped directly to corresponding ESRS topic areas. For example, ESRS chapter E1 on climate change and energy corresponds to multiple VSME chapters, ensuring that SMEs reporting under the VSME produce information that is intelligible and useful to large companies under the CSRD [[esrs]]. This alignment facilitates integration between the voluntary and mandatory regimes, allowing SMEs to contribute to broader sustainability goals while meeting the expectations of their larger counterparts.

The CSRD also influences the development of the VSME, as the need for SMEs to provide sustainability data to large companies under the CSRD was a key driver behind the creation of the VSME framework [[csrd]]. This interplay between the CSRD and VSME highlights the importance of a structured sustainability strategy for SMEs, which can generate value through customer retention, personnel attraction, and improved access to capital [[vsme]].

## Conclusion

For SMEs, the choice between voluntary reporting under the VSME and mandatory reporting under the ESRS/CSRD depends on their size, regulatory context, and strategic goals. While the ESRS/CSRD impose comprehensive and legally binding requirements, the VSME offers a proportionate and accessible alternative that supports SMEs in communicating sustainability information effectively. The integration of these regimes ensures that SMEs can contribute to broader sustainability objectives while navigating the evolving regulatory landscape.
