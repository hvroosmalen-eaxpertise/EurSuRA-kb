# Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)

The **Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)** was established in 2015 by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) to develop voluntary, consistent climate-related financial risk disclosures. Its recommendations, published in 2017, became the global reference for how companies communicate climate-related risks and opportunities to investors and other financial stakeholders.

In 2023, the ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board) assumed responsibility for monitoring TCFD adoption after the publication of [[IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures]], which incorporates and supersedes the TCFD recommendations.

## The Four Pillars

The TCFD framework is organised around four thematic areas that reflect how organisations operate:

### 1. Governance

Disclose the organisation's governance around climate-related risks and opportunities.

- Board oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities
- Management's role in assessing and managing climate-related risks and opportunities

### 2. Strategy

Disclose the actual and potential impacts of climate-related risks and opportunities on the organisation's business, strategy, and financial planning.

- Climate-related risks and opportunities identified over short, medium, and long time horizons
- Impact on business, strategy, and financial planning
- Resilience of strategy, using scenario analysis (including a 2°C or lower scenario)

### 3. Risk Management

Disclose how the organisation identifies, assesses, and manages climate-related risks.

- Processes for identifying and assessing climate-related risks
- Processes for managing climate-related risks
- How these processes are integrated into overall risk management

### 4. Metrics and Targets

Disclose the metrics and targets used to assess and manage relevant climate-related risks and opportunities.

- Metrics used to assess climate-related risks and opportunities
- Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions
- Targets used to manage climate-related risks and opportunities, and performance against targets

## Relationship to ESRS E1

The [[ESRS — European Sustainability Reporting Standards|ESRS]] E1 (Climate Change) standard explicitly aligns with the TCFD pillars. ESRS E1 disclosure requirements map directly onto the four TCFD areas, with additional requirements on transition plans and gross emissions. Companies already reporting under TCFD will find the ESRS E1 structure familiar but more prescriptive and detailed.

## Relationship to IFRS S2

The ISSB's **IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures** (2023) is the formal successor to the TCFD recommendations. IFRS S2 incorporates the four-pillar structure and adds mandatory industry-based metrics. Many jurisdictions outside the EU are adopting IFRS S2 as their mandatory climate disclosure standard.

## Physical and Transition Risk

TCFD introduced the widely-used distinction between:

- **Physical risks** — risks arising from climate change itself (acute: extreme weather events; chronic: sea-level rise, temperature shifts)
- **Transition risks** — risks arising from the shift to a low-carbon economy (policy, legal, technology, market, reputational)

This taxonomy is embedded in ESRS E1 and used across EU sustainability reporting.
