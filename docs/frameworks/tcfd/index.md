# TCFD — Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures

The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) was established by the Financial Stability Board in December 2015, chaired by Michael Bloomberg, and published its final recommendations in June 2017. It was designed to give investors, lenders and insurers a consistent, decision-useful view of the climate-related financial risks and opportunities facing publicly listed companies, financial institutions and other major issuers. In October 2023 the FSB formally transferred TCFD's monitoring responsibilities to the IFRS Foundation; from 1 January 2024 the TCFD itself was wound down and its recommendations live on inside IFRS S2 and ESRS E1.

TCFD was a framework rather than a standard: non-prescriptive, principle-based, aimed at mainstream financial filings rather than stand-alone sustainability reports. That framing is one reason its uptake was fast — more than 4 000 supporters across 100-plus jurisdictions by 2023 — and why its vocabulary now sits inside every major climate-disclosure regime.

## The Four Pillars and the Eleven Recommended Disclosures

The TCFD architecture organises climate-related disclosure around four pillars, each with recommended disclosures:

1. **Governance**
    1. Board oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities.
    2. Management's role in assessing and managing those risks and opportunities.
2. **Strategy**
    1. Climate-related risks and opportunities identified over the short, medium and long term.
    2. Their impact on the business, strategy and financial planning.
    3. Resilience of the strategy under different climate-related scenarios, including a 2 °C-or-lower scenario.
3. **Risk management**
    1. Processes for identifying and assessing climate-related risks.
    2. Processes for managing climate-related risks.
    3. Integration of these processes into the organisation's overall risk management.
4. **Metrics and targets**
    1. Metrics used to assess climate-related risks and opportunities, consistent with the strategy and risk-management processes.
    2. Scope 1, [[Scope 2]] and — where appropriate — [[Scope 3]] greenhouse-gas emissions, and the related risks.
    3. Targets used to manage climate-related risks and opportunities, and performance against them.

## Physical and Transition Risks

TCFD introduced the two-category risk taxonomy that is now the common language of climate reporting:

- **Physical risks** — acute (storms, floods, wildfires) and chronic (sea-level rise, chronic heat, water stress).
- **Transition risks** — policy and legal, technology, market, and reputational risks arising from the shift to a lower-carbon economy.

Opportunities fall into resource efficiency, energy source, products and services, markets, and resilience.

## Scenario Analysis

The TCFD recommendation that proved hardest in practice is **strategy c**: resilience under different climate scenarios. TCFD asked organisations to describe how their strategy performs under both an orderly transition to 2 °C or lower, and a scenario of continued high emissions and physical disruption. The 2021 TCFD Guidance on Metrics, Targets, and Transition Plans expanded this with practical guidance on scenario selection, time horizons and quantitative analysis. The IPCC's shared socio-economic pathways (SSPs), the IEA's Net Zero Emissions scenario and the NGFS scenarios became the common references.

## Transition Plans

Beginning with the 2021 guidance update, TCFD placed transition plans at the centre of its strategy disclosure. A credible transition plan sets out the strategic actions the organisation will take, the targets it will meet (including interim milestones), the capital it will deploy, and how it will govern and monitor the transition. This architecture is the direct ancestor of the ESRS E1-1 transition plan disclosure and the Article 22 climate transition plan under the [[Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)]].

## From TCFD to IFRS S2

[[IFRS S2]] *Climate-related Disclosures* was published in June 2023 and incorporates the TCFD recommendations in full — the four pillars, the eleven disclosures, scenario analysis, transition plans, and the industry-based metrics (which drew on SASB). IFRS S2 goes further than TCFD in several places:

- mandates Scope 1, 2 and 3 GHG emissions disclosure, measured using the [[Greenhouse Gas Protocol (GHG Protocol)]];
- requires disclosure of the amount and percentage of assets or activities vulnerable to transition risks, to physical risks, and aligned with climate opportunities;
- requires disclosure of internal carbon pricing;
- requires disclosure of the connection between executive remuneration and climate performance;
- mandates scenario analysis rather than merely recommending it.

For a company that already reports to TCFD, moving to IFRS S2 is a disclosure-tightening rather than a methodology change.

## TCFD and ESRS E1

Within the EU, ESRS E1 "Climate Change" incorporates the TCFD architecture and extends it with [[Double Materiality]] (impact as well as financial), climate transition-plan datapoints, and detailed GHG metrics (gross and net, with removals, and separate reporting of credits used for offsetting). A company reporting under ESRS E1 produces, as a by-product, the data it would need for IFRS S2 and TCFD.

## Jurisdictional Mandates

TCFD-aligned disclosure was mandated for premium-listed companies in the United Kingdom from 2021 and for large companies and LLPs from 2022; Japan requires TCFD-aligned disclosure through its Corporate Governance Code; New Zealand introduced mandatory climate-related disclosures for large financial institutions and listed issuers from 2023; Singapore, Hong Kong, Switzerland and Brazil followed with phased requirements. Most of these mandates are now being updated to point to IFRS S1 / S2 directly.

## Implications for SMEs

[[SMEs]] are not direct subjects of TCFD-based mandates. They encounter TCFD vocabulary through two channels:

- **Lender and investor questionnaires.** Banks, especially large European and Japanese banks subject to TCFD-aligned supervisory expectations, ask SME borrowers for governance of climate risk, physical risk exposure (location data), and transition plan intent.
- **Large-buyer supply-chain requests.** TCFD-reporting corporates increasingly ask tier-1 suppliers for Scope 1 and 2 emissions, electricity source and climate-governance information.

The [[Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs (VSME)]] Comprehensive module's climate datapoints are deliberately mappable onto TCFD metrics-and-targets expectations.
