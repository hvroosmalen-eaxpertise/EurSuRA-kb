# UN Sustainable Development Goals

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are the seventeen goals of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, adopted by all 193 UN Member States at the UN Sustainable Development Summit in September 2015 (Resolution A/RES/70/1, *Transforming our World*). They succeeded the Millennium Development Goals (2000–2015) and run from 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2030. The SDGs are an indivisible framework: environmental, social and economic objectives are deliberately treated as interlinked, and progress on any single goal is understood to depend on progress across the others.

The seventeen goals are underpinned by 169 targets and a Global Indicator Framework that, after refinements, comprises 231 unique indicators maintained by the UN Statistical Commission's Inter-agency and Expert Group on SDG Indicators (IAEG-SDGs). The framework is the authoritative reference for sustainability outcomes used by governments, international finance institutions, companies, and standard-setters.

## The Seventeen Goals

| # | Goal | Short gloss |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | No Poverty | End poverty in all its forms everywhere |
| 2 | Zero Hunger | Food security, improved nutrition, sustainable agriculture |
| 3 | Good Health and Well-being | Healthy lives at all ages |
| 4 | Quality Education | Inclusive, equitable, lifelong learning |
| 5 | Gender Equality | Empowerment of all women and girls |
| 6 | Clean Water and Sanitation | Availability and sustainable management |
| 7 | Affordable and Clean Energy | Reliable, sustainable, modern energy for all |
| 8 | Decent Work and Economic Growth | Productive employment and decent work |
| 9 | Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure | Resilient infrastructure, sustainable industrialisation |
| 10 | Reduced Inequalities | Within and among countries |

| 17 | Partnerships for the Goals | Means of implementation and global partnership |

## Governance and Monitoring

Progress is tracked by the UN Statistics Division's annual *Sustainable Development Goals Report* and discussed at the UN High-Level Political Forum (HLPF) each July. Member States submit Voluntary National Reviews (VNRs); more than 190 VNRs have been presented since 2016.

A dedicated UN body, the UN Statistical Commission's Inter-agency and Expert Group on SDG Indicators (IAEG-SDGs), maintains the Global Indicator Framework. Indicators are tiered by methodological maturity, and the framework is periodically refined — the latest comprehensive review was in 2025.

## The Three Dimensions and the "Five Ps"

The 2030 Agenda frames the Goals around three dimensions (economic, social and environmental) and the "Five Ps":

- **People** — ending poverty and hunger in all forms and ensuring dignity and equality.
- **Planet** — protecting natural resources and climate for future generations.
- **Prosperity** — ensuring prosperous and fulfilling lives in harmony with nature.
- **Peace** — fostering peaceful, just and inclusive societies.
- **Partnership** — mobilising the means to implement the Agenda through a revitalised Global Partnership.

## Business and the SDGs

The SDGs are a public-sector framework, but the 2030 Agenda explicitly calls on business as a key partner. The UN Global Compact, the GRI and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development jointly published the *SDG Compass* (2015), the standard methodology for aligning corporate strategy and reporting with the Goals:

1. Understanding the SDGs.
2. Defining priorities (impact mapping, materiality, prioritisation).
3. Setting goals (ambition, scope, key performance indicators).
4. Integrating (anchoring in strategy, embedding across functions, engaging in partnerships).
5. Reporting and communicating.

Related business-oriented guidance includes the *SDG Industry Matrix* series, the *Business Reporting on the SDGs* standard (GRI / UN Global Compact, 2017–2021), and the *SDG Impact Standards* for enterprises, bond issuers and private-equity funds (UNDP, 2021).

## SDG-Washing

As SDG referencing became ubiquitous in corporate reports, concern grew about **SDG-washing**: the practice of mapping existing activities onto SDG icons without rigour, without baseline measurement, and without accounting for negative impacts. Credible SDG reporting now expects:

- a prioritisation that identifies the goals the organisation most affects (positive and negative), not those easiest to communicate;
- quantitative indicators tied to UN Global Indicator Framework indicators or to credible proxies;
- disclosure of adverse impacts on goals, not only contributions; and
- integration into mainstream disclosure under [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)]], [[GRI]], or [[International Sustainability Standards Board]] standards, where the SDGs are referenced as outcomes rather than as a parallel disclosure track.

## Interactions with Reporting Standards and Regulation

- **ESRS.** ESRS 2 General Disclosures explicitly invites reporting on how an organisation's sustainability strategy contributes to the SDGs, and material topic disclosures should reflect SDG linkages.
- **GRI.** GRI 3 Material Topics 2021 and the SDG Compass are directly aligned; GRI publishes an SDG-GRI mapping.
- **ISSB.** IFRS S1 and S2 are financial-materiality standards and do not require SDG disclosure, but SASB-based industry metrics correlate with many SDG indicators.
- **EU Taxonomy / SFDR.** Several of the EU Taxonomy's six environmental objectives map directly to SDGs 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15.
- **UN Guiding Principles.** The UNGPs underpin SDG 8 (decent work), SDG 10 (reduced inequalities) and SDG 16 (peace and strong institutions).

## Implications for SMEs

An SME rarely has the resources to publish a stand-alone SDG report, but SDG framing is lightweight when done pragmatically:

- Identify the three to five goals the business most affects, including any goal where the business has a negative impact (SDG 12 or SDG 8 are typical candidates).
- Pick one measurable indicator per prioritised goal — ideally one already collected for VSME, ESRS or GRI reporting.
- Report in the organisation's existing sustainability statement, using the official SDG icons under the UN Communications Materials guidance.
