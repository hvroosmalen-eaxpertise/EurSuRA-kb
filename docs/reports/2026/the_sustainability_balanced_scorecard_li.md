# Sustainability Balanced Scorecard

The Sustainability Balanced Scorecard (SBSC) is a strategic management tool that integrates environmental and social aspects into a firm's overall management system. It extends the traditional Balanced Scorecard (BSC) framework, originally developed by Kaplan and Norton, to incorporate sustainability considerations. The SBSC aims to align corporate strategies with sustainability goals by linking operational activities to long-term strategic objectives through causal relationships.

The BSC, introduced in the early 1990s, was designed to address limitations in traditional performance measurement systems by incorporating multiple perspectives. These include financial, customer, internal processes, and learning and growth. The SBSC builds upon this framework by integrating the three pillars of sustainability—environmental, social, and economic—into a unified strategic management approach. This allows firms to evaluate and manage their sustainability performance alongside traditional financial metrics.

The SBSC is structured around four perspectives, each representing a different dimension of organizational performance. The financial perspective assesses the economic outcomes of strategic initiatives, while the customer perspective focuses on market competitiveness and customer satisfaction. The internal process perspective evaluates the efficiency and effectiveness of internal operations, and the learning and growth perspective emphasizes employee development and organizational capabilities. These perspectives are interconnected through cause-and-effect relationships, enabling firms to identify key performance drivers and strategic priorities.

The integration of sustainability into the BSC framework is particularly important given the increasing recognition of environmental and social issues in business decision-making. While traditional management systems often treat these aspects separately, the SBSC provides a mechanism for aligning sustainability goals with economic objectives. This integration is essential for achieving long-term sustainability, as it ensures that environmental and social performance is directly linked to the firm's economic success.

The development of the SBSC involves a structured process of defining strategic objectives, selecting appropriate performance indicators, and establishing causal relationships between these indicators and the firm's strategic goals. This process is guided by the principles of strategic management, emphasizing the importance of top-down communication and alignment across all organizational levels.

The SBSC has been discussed in academic literature as a promising approach for integrating sustainability into corporate strategy. It is seen as a way to overcome the limitations of conventional management systems, which often fail to account for the long-term impacts of environmental and social decisions. By incorporating sustainability into the core of strategic management, the SBSC supports firms in achieving a more holistic and sustainable business model.
