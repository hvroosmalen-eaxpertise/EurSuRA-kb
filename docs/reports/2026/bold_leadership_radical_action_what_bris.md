--- # Bold Leadership, Radical Action: What Bristol Residents Want on Climate Change Bristol residents express a strong concern about climate change, with a significant emphasis on the need for radical action and citywide leadership. This research, conducted in 2020, aimed to understand how ordinary people in Bristol perceive climate change, how it affects their lives, and what they expect from local policies. The findings highlight the importance of equity, the urgency of transport-related changes, and the role of the One City Climate Strategy in shaping the city's response to the climate emergency. This report is based on interviews and an online survey conducted with 333 residents in person and 1343 online, covering a wide range of backgrounds and experiences. The study was funded by the UK Research and Innovation Quality-Related Strategic Priorities Fund and supported by the University of Bristol and local organizations. The research team included eight researchers, with contributions from postgraduate and undergraduate students, and was led by Emilia Melville, Jack Nicholls, and Hen Wilkinson. ## Background The research was initiated to gather insights from Bristol residents about their views on climate change and its impacts, with the goal of informing strategic leadership teams ahead of the Mayoral elections in 2020. Although the elections were postponed due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the research continued through lockdown, shifting focus to include residents' perspectives on mitigation strategies. The study was supported by the Bristol Inclusive Economy Initiative and the School of Geographical Sciences, and provided an opportunity to train and support a team of eight University of Bristol students in the research process. ## Research Approach The research team, consisting of three senior researchers connected to the University of Bristol and two local organizations, conducted face-to-face interviews with residents across all Bristol postcodes. The interviews, which lasted approximately 10 minutes, aimed to understand how residents perceive and talk about climate change, their specific concerns, and their views on local policies. The team also developed an online survey to collect additional data, which was promoted through local media outlets. The survey included an additional question about the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on residents' views on climate change. ## What Residents Think ### How Climate Change Is Framed Bristol residents generally frame climate change as a serious issue that causes fear, sadness, and anger. Only 20 residents questioned the reality of climate change, indicating a widespread consensus that it is happening and is worrying the local population. Residents connected with the topic through various points, including extreme weather events and the destruction of livelihoods, but generally spoke of their fear of global large-scale impacts such as the melting icecaps, the extinction of polar bears, or changes in weather patterns. ### Awareness of Local Policies Residents showed varying levels of awareness of local policies related to climate change. While some were well-informed about the city's climate policies, others expressed a lack of knowledge or understanding. The research highlighted the importance of clear communication and transparency in policy implementation to ensure that residents are informed and engaged. ### How Responses Differed Across the City The research found that responses to climate change varied across different parts of Bristol. Residents in the city center expressed more concern about transport and urban development, while those in the outskirts focused on issues such as housing and infrastructure. These differences reflect the diverse experiences and priorities of residents in different areas of the city. ### Visions for the Future Residents expressed a desire for a more sustainable and equitable future, with a focus on reducing carbon emissions, improving public transportation, and promoting renewable energy. Many emphasized the need for radical action and a commitment to addressing the climate emergency at the city level. ### Impact of Covid-19 on Research Responses The pandemic had a significant impact on residents' views on climate change. Many reported that the experience of lockdown and the shift to remote work and living had changed their perspectives on sustainability and environmental responsibility. The online survey revealed that a substantial number of residents felt that the pandemic had shifted their views on climate change, with a greater emphasis on local action and community engagement. ### Residentsâ€™ Most Talked-About Topics The most discussed topics among residents included transport, housing, and the role of local government in addressing climate change.
