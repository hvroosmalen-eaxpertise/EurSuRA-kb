# Strategies and Practices for Sustainability: Experiences from Firm Level

Strategies and practices for sustainability have been explored through various approaches, including backcasting and scenario planning, to integrate sustainability into corporate strategy. This paper discusses the experiences of firms in implementing these strategies and the challenges they face in aligning with the demands of a sustainable future. The work is based on an action research project conducted at three large Swedish corporations in 2004.

The concept of sustainability has gained increasing attention since the publication of Carson's (1962) seminal work *Silent Spring* and the Brundtland Commission's (1987) report *Our Common Future*. While sustainability has been a public concern, its integration into corporate strategy has been less consistent. However, the actions of industrial and service firms significantly impact sustainability through their production processes, resource use, and influence on product lifecycle in society. Additionally, firms affect social domains such as health, child labor, and social equity.

Inspired by the Brundtland Commission, several approaches have been developed to analyze the needs for a sustainable society. The Natural Step, for example, has influenced how firms develop their strategies since the early 1990s. Holmberg (1998) outlined a backcasting approach to strategy development based on system conditions for sustainability. Other researchers have focused on ecological auditing and corporate social responsibility (CSR), while more recent work by Porter and Kramer (2006) emphasizes the integration of social and environmental issues into core corporate strategy to create shared value.

Various tools have been used to incorporate sustainability into strategy development, including scenario planning, which has been widely applied in industries such as oil. Scenario planning involves creating plausible future scenarios to guide strategic decision-making, often based on a deductive approach with four equally possible developments. This method aims to develop strategies that are robust across different scenarios, although it has been noted that such strategies may yield mediocre results rather than standout performance.

In our approach, the four system conditions for sustainability (Holmberg 1995) have been used as a guiding framework for strategy development in combination with scenario planning to enhance robustness. These system conditions are timeless and applicable both in the long term and as guiding principles for current decision-making. They provide relative and system-related guidelines rather than absolute restrictions, focusing on ecological sustainability through the potential damaging effects of systematic increases in substances or degradation of the environment.

The integration of system conditions with scenario planning allows firms to consider a multitude of factors that could affect their development over a selected time period, typically 10–15 years. This combination helps firms to anticipate and adapt to future uncertainties, ensuring that their strategies align with the principles of sustainability.
