# OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct

The **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct** (hereinafter "the Guidelines") are a set of government-backed recommendations addressed to [[multinational enterprises]] (MNEs) operating in or from adhering countries, designed to promote responsible business conduct and [[sustainable development]].[^1] First adopted in 1976 and most recently updated in 2023, the Guidelines represent the most comprehensive multilateral instrument for [[corporate responsibility]] endorsed by governments.[^2] Their implementation is supported by a network of National Contact Points for Responsible Business Conduct (NCPs), established by adhering governments to promote the Guidelines' effectiveness.[^1]

---

## Background

### Origins and Development

The Guidelines were introduced in 1976 as part of the OECD Declaration on International Investment and Multinational Enterprises (OECD/LEGAL/0144).[^1] They have been revised periodically to reflect evolving social, environmental, and technological conditions affecting the international business environment.[^1] A significant update was undertaken in 2011; the most recent revision, adopted in 2023, incorporates approximately a decade of implementation experience accumulated since that revision.[^1]

The 2023 amendments were adopted by the 51 Subscribing Countries to the OECD Declaration on International Investment and Multinational Enterprises, together with the European Union, acting through the OECD Working Party on Responsible Business Conduct and the OECD Investment Committee.[^1] The update process included two public consultations open to stakeholders worldwide, as well as close engagement with the institutional stakeholders Business at OECD, the Trade Union Advisory Committee (TUAC) to the OECD, and OECD Watch, which collectively represent the positions of millions of enterprises, workers, and civil society members globally.[^1]

### Legal Status and Governance

The Guidelines are recommendations rather than binding legal obligations.[^1] The Council Decision on the Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct (OECD/LEGAL/0307) was adopted by the OECD Council at ministerial level.[^2] Commentaries on both instruments were approved by the OECD Investment Committee.[^2] The Centre for Responsible Business Conduct of the OECD Directorate for Financial and Enterprise Affairs provides ongoing secretariat support.[^1]

---

## Scope and Subscribing Countries

As of 8 June 2023, all OECD member countries are Subscribing Countries, along with Argentina, Brazil, Bulgaria, Croatia, Egypt, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Morocco, Peru, Romania, Tunisia, Ukraine, and Uruguay.[^2] The European Community has been invited to participate in the component relating to National Treatment for matters within its competence.[^2]

The Guidelines apply to MNEs operating on or from the territory of Subscribing Countries.[^2] They do not affect the right of Subscribing Countries to regulate the admission of foreign investment or the conditions of establishment for foreign enterprises.[^2]

---

## Key Principles

### Declaration on International Investment and Multinational Enterprises

Subscribing governments affirm, through the Declaration, that international investment is of major importance to the world economy and that MNEs play a significant role in the investment process.[^2] The Declaration addresses four interconnected instruments:

1. **Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises** – recommendations for responsible business conduct;
2. **National Treatment** – a commitment by Subscribing Countries to accord enterprises operating on their territory and owned or controlled by nationals of another Subscribing Country treatment no less favourable than that accorded to domestic enterprises in like situations, consistent with international law;[^2]
3. **Conflicting Requirements** – a commitment to cooperate in avoiding or minimising the imposition of conflicting requirements on MNEs;[^2]
4. **International Investment Incentives and Impediments** – recognition of the need to strengthen cooperation on international direct investment.[^2]

## 2023 Updates

The 2023 revision introduced several substantive changes to the Guidelines, as described in the OECD foreword:[^1]

- **Climate change and biodiversity**: New recommendations for enterprises to align their activities with internationally agreed objectives on climate change and biodiversity.
- **Technology and data**: Inclusion of [[due diligence]] expectations with respect to the development, financing, sale, licensing, trade, and use of technology, including the collection and use of data.
- **Product and service impacts**: Recommendations on how enterprises are expected to apply due diligence in relation to impacts and business relationships connected with the use of their products and services.
- **Protection of at-risk persons**: Enhanced protection for persons and groups at risk, including individuals who raise concerns about enterprise conduct (commonly referred to as [[whistleblowers]]).
- **Responsible business conduct disclosure**: Updated recommendations on corporate reporting and disclosure practices.
- **Anti-corruption**: Expanded due diligence recommendations covering all forms of corruption.
- **Lobbying**: New recommendations requiring enterprises to ensure that their lobbying activities are consistent with the Guidelines.
- **National Contact Points**: Strengthened procedures to ensure the visibility, effectiveness, and functional equivalence of NCPs across Subscribing Countries.

---

## Implementation Mechanism

### National Contact Points

NCPs are bodies established by Subscribing Country governments to promote awareness of, and compliance with, the Guidelines.[^1] The 2023 revision strengthened procedural requirements for NCPs to improve their functional equivalence—meaning that NCP processes and outcomes should be comparable across different jurisdictions.[^1]

### Council Decision

The Council Decision on the Guidelines (OECD/LEGAL/0307) sets out the formal procedures governing the Guidelines' implementation, including the roles of the Investment Committee and NCPs.[^2] The Decision was adopted at ministerial level, giving it a high degree of political authority within the OECD governance framework.[^2]

## Relationship to Other Instruments

Multiple OECD committees contributed to the 2023 thematic chapter revisions, including the Competition Committee; the Consumer Policy Committee; the Corporate Governance Committee; the Development Assistance Committee; the Digital Economy Committee; the Employment, Labour and Social Affairs Committee; the Environment Policy Committee; the Financial Markets Committee; the Tax Committee; the Committee on Industry, Innovation and Entrepreneurship; the Insurance and Private Pensions Committee; the Committee for Scientific and Technological Policy; the Small and Medium-sized Enterprises and Entrepreneurship Committee; the Trade Committee; the Working Group on Bribery in International Business Transactions; and the Working Group of Senior Public Integrity Officials.[^1]
