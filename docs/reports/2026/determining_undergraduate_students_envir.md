# Determining Undergraduate Students’ Environmental Awareness and Environmental Sensitivity

Undergraduate students’ environmental awareness and sensitivity were assessed in a study conducted at Hacettepe University, Turkey, involving 224 students from the Department of Family and Consumer Sciences. The study aimed to evaluate the level of environmental awareness and sensitivity among these students and to examine how these levels vary by demographic factors such as gender, parental educational status, and income level. The findings indicate that students’ environmental awareness and sensitivity are generally above average, with significant variations observed across different demographic groups [^1].

## Background

The environment is defined as the biological, physical, social, economic, and cultural surroundings in which living beings maintain their relationships and interact throughout their lives [^2]. Environmental issues such as deforestation, erosion, rapid population growth, and pollution have become global concerns, prompting the development of environmental policies and legal frameworks in many countries, including Turkey [^3]. Environmental sensitivity, which refers to the willingness to take positive actions against environmental issues, has evolved over time and has influenced the formation of environmental policies and regulations [^4].

Environmental awareness encompasses an individual's understanding of the social, historical, and natural environment, participation in decision-making processes related to environmental problems, and a commitment to protecting natural resources without causing harm [^5]. It is considered a fundamental aspect of contemporary human behavior, reflecting principles of equality and justice in the context of basic human rights [^6]. The development of environmental awareness and sensitivity is influenced by various factors, including family, education, media, and non-governmental organizations [^7].

## Environmental Awareness and Sensitivity

The study used a 5-point Likert scale to measure students’ environmental awareness and sensitivity, with responses ranging from "I strongly disagree" (1) to "I strongly agree" (5). The results showed that the average score for environmental awareness was 3.50, indicating a level higher than average, while the average score for environmental sensitivity was 3.80, suggesting a significantly higher level of sensitivity compared to awareness [^8]. 

The study also found that environmental awareness and sensitivity levels varied significantly by gender, parental educational status, and income level (p < 0.01). Specifically, female students, students with parents who had higher educational attainment, and students from higher-income households demonstrated higher levels of environmental awareness and sensitivity [^9].

## Methodology

The research involved 224 undergraduate students from Hacettepe University’s Department of Family and Consumer Sciences. Data were collected through face-to-face interviews conducted in May–June 2014, using a structured questionnaire. The questionnaire consisted of two parts: the first part included demographic questions and questions related to environmental knowledge, while the second part used the Environmental Awareness and Sensitivity Scale [^10]. 

The data were analyzed using SPSS 21.0 for Windows. Demographic findings were presented using frequency and percentage distributions, while scale items were analyzed using percentage distributions, arithmetic mean, and standard deviation statistics. For comparisons of environmental awareness and sensitivity levels across different demographic groups, a t-test was used for two groups and analysis of variance (ANOVA) for more than two groups [^11].

## Results

The study revealed that 51% of the participants were female, and 49% were male. A significant proportion of students (31.1%) were in their second year of study, and 66% belonged to nuclear families. The majority of students (35%) had mothers who were high school graduates, and 45% had fathers who were university graduates. Monthly income levels varied, with 40% of students reporting an income between 2501 and 3500 Turkish Lira (TL). 

Only 90% of students received no extracurricular environmental education, and 95% were not members of any non-governmental organizations related to environmental issues. Furthermore, 96% of students did not follow any periodical publications related to the environment. Among the sources of environmental information, students most frequently cited their school (43.0%), followed by family (39.0%), the internet (37.0%), and radio and television (9.1%) [^12].

The average score for environmental awareness was reported as 3.50, while the average score for environmental sensitivity was 3.80. The highest scores were observed for statements such as "I take care to use both sides of the pages on which I write" (4.72) and "I pay attention to the trash in my hand reaching the garbage cans when I am indoors other than my house such as school/hospital/shopping centre, etc." (3.94). In contrast, the lowest score was recorded for the statement "Even if I have my own car, usually I use public transportation in order to minimize air pollution" (2.96) [^13].
