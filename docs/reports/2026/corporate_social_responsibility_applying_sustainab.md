# Corporate Social Responsibility: Applying Sustainability Principles in Stakeholder Engagement

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) has become a significant aspect of business operations, particularly in the context of stakeholder engagement and sustainability. In South Africa, the corporate sector has increasingly adopted CSR initiatives to address socio-economic challenges, with notable investments in community development and social programs. The total CSR expenditure in South Africa was estimated at R8.2 billion in 2013/2014 [^1]. Despite these efforts, the voices of local communities remain underrepresented in decision-making processes, which are critical for effective change management [^1].

## Background

The concept of CSR has evolved over decades, shaped by global socio-economic development discussions and the recognition of its potential to contribute to inclusive growth and poverty alleviation [^1]. Initially, CSR was framed around economic responsibilities, with legal, ethical, and discretionary obligations forming a hierarchical structure [^1]. However, contemporary challenges have elevated ethical considerations to a foundational level for business success, aligning them with economic and legal responsibilities [^1].

In South Africa, CSR emerged as a response to the socio-economic imbalances left by apartheid. Following the country's transition to democracy in 1994, the government initiated various social projects to address historical inequalities. This period also saw the private sector increasingly embracing CSR initiatives, driven by the need for transparency and ethical governance [^1].

## Corporate Social Responsibility Development in South Africa

South Africa, with its 10th largest stock market globally, is a key indicator of economic prospects for sub-Saharan Africa [^1]. The country's CSR landscape has been influenced by both domestic and international frameworks, including the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD) and the United Nations Global Compact [^1]. These frameworks have encouraged South African businesses to contribute to sustainability and development initiatives.

The practice of CSR extends corporate obligations to include multiple stakeholders, such as shareholders, employees, communities, and the environment [^1]. In South Africa, corporate social investment (CSI) programs have become critical, addressing issues like healthcare, education, housing, and youth development [^1]. These programs aim to create shared value by delivering positive, sustainable, and measurable benefits to both communities and the companies themselves [^1].

## Legislative Framework

The Companies Act 71 of 2008 (Section 7) in South Africa has influenced business practices by emphasizing ethical considerations in organizational operations [^1]. This legislation has shifted the focus from profit-centric models to a more holistic approach that includes corporate governance, CSR, and Black Economic Empowerment (BEE) practices [^1].

The BEE policy, introduced in 1994, serves as an affirmative action strategy to address historical economic disparities and promote the inclusion of black South Africans in the economy [^1]. This policy has been instrumental in fostering economic growth and creating opportunities for black business ownership through direct and indirect empowerment mechanisms [^1].
