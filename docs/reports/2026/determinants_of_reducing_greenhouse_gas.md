--- # Determinants of Reducing Greenhouse Gas Emissions in European Union Countries Greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions reduction is a critical goal for the European Union (EU) and globally, as it is essential for balancing economic development, environmental protection, and societal expectations. This article examines the determinants of GHG emissions reduction in EU countries, focusing on the role of environmental policy and "green taxes" as instruments of tax policy. The study employs linear regression analysis to identify key factors influencing GHG emissions and assesses whether green taxes significantly contribute to emission reductions. ## Background The industrial growth of the 19th and 20th centuries led to increased GHG emissions and other atmospheric pollutants, contributing to global warming and climate change. International organizations and governments have since pursued policies to mitigate these effects. While various instruments, such as tradable emission quotas and carbon accounting systems, have been proposed, the role of green taxesâ€”also known as environmental taxesâ€”has remained a subject of debate. These taxes are designed to influence behavior toward sustainability by imposing costs on activities that generate GHG emissions. However, their effectiveness in reducing emissions is not universally accepted, and their impact is often influenced by broader economic, social, and political factors. ## Determinants of Greenhouse Gas Emissions The study identifies several key determinants of GHG emissions, including population size, current and capital transfers for environmental protection, and the supply, transformation, and consumption of solid fossil fuels. These factors are analyzed using multiple linear regression models, which predict GHG emissions based on independent variables derived from the literature. The analysis excludes COâ‚‚ emissions trading due to data limitations. The regression models are conducted using SPSS, a specialized statistical software package. The findings indicate that green taxes are not a significant determinant of GHG emission reductions compared to other factors. Instead, changes in consumer behavior, influenced by the non-fiscal functions of taxes, appear to be a critical factor in reducing emissions. Public authorities are encouraged to promote behaviors that reduce emissions through incentives rather than relying primarily on taxation or fiscal incentives. ## Environmental Taxes and Sustainable Development Environmental taxes are recognized as an important tool for influencing behavior toward sustainability. They can create incentives for businesses to invest in low-carbon technologies and alternative fuels. However, the effectiveness of these taxes may be limited by existing mechanisms for allocating free emission allowances. The increasing cost of "brown" energy, particularly due to energy taxes, has led to a substitution effect, reducing demand for polluting energy sources. This effect is closely associated with non-financial factors such as environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations, particularly social responsibility for climate change. The literature highlights the variability of normative information's impact across different climate mitigation contexts, depending on familiarity and behavioral factors. New regulations on social and environmental risk management are becoming increasingly important in the context of climate responsibility and are linked to the non-fiscal functions of taxes. ## Conclusion The study concludes that green taxes play a significant role in environmental policy and that their abolition could lead to increased GHG emissions. While green taxes are primarily seen as a fiscal instrument, their potential non-fiscal functions in promoting sustainable behavior are gaining recognition.
