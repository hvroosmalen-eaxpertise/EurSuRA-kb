# Corporate Sustainability Strategies

Corporate sustainability strategies are essential for organizations aiming to integrate sustainability into their operations effectively. This paper explores the development of specific sustainability profiles and maturity levels to guide companies in implementing sustainable practices. The study highlights the importance of aligning corporate strategies with sustainability goals, emphasizing the need for a structured approach to sustainability management.

## Background

Sustainable development gained prominence through the Brundtland Report (World Commission on Environment and Development, 1987), which defined it as a process to achieve sustainability by balancing economic, ecological, and social dimensions. Corporate sustainability, a subset of sustainable development, involves integrating these dimensions into organizational practices. The paper addresses the gap between corporate sustainability efforts and the implementation of coherent strategies, proposing a framework to enhance the effectiveness of sustainability initiatives.

## Corporate Sustainability Strategies

Corporate sustainability strategies can be categorized into different types, each with distinct focuses and approaches. These include:

- **Introverted – Risk Mitigation Strategy**: Focuses on legal and external standards to mitigate risks related to environmental and social aspects.
- **Extroverted – Legitimizing Strategy**: Emphasizes external relationships and the license to operate.
- **Conservative – Efficiency Strategy**: Concentrates on eco-efficiency and cleaner production.
- **Visionary – Holistic Sustainability Strategy**: Encompasses sustainability issues across all business activities, deriving competitive advantages through innovation and differentiation.

These strategies are designed to address sustainability challenges by integrating various aspects of corporate operations. An extroverted strategy can be transformative, actively changing market conditions and creating new opportunities aligned with sustainable development principles.

## Sustainability Aspects

To develop effective sustainability strategies, it is crucial to understand the range of sustainability issues that must be addressed. These issues are often categorized into three dimensions: economic, ecological, and social. The paper outlines key sustainability aspects within each dimension, providing a framework for organizations to implement sustainable practices.

### Economic Dimension of Corporate Sustainability

The economic dimension of corporate sustainability is often referred to as the 'generic dimension' (Baedeker et al., 2002; DJSI, 2007). It encompasses aspects that are essential for an organization's long-term market viability, alongside environmental and social considerations. Key aspects include innovation and technology, collaboration, knowledge management, processes, purchase, and sustainability reporting. These aspects are critical for achieving both financial and sustainability outcomes.

### Social Dimension of Corporate Sustainability

The social dimension of corporate sustainability involves the organization's responsibility and commitment to its actions, particularly in business activities. This includes efforts in sustainability-related research and development to reduce environmental impacts, collaboration with various stakeholders, knowledge management, and integrating sustainability into daily operations. The social dimension also emphasizes the consideration of sustainability issues in procurement and reporting practices.
