# Voluntary SME Standard (VSME)

## Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for Non-Listed Micro, Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises

The **Voluntary Standard for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises** (VSME) is a sustainability reporting standard published by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG) to support non-listed micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in disclosing sustainability-related information.[^1] The standard is voluntary in nature and is designed to be proportionate to the fundamental characteristics of smaller enterprises, while addressing the same sustainability topics covered by the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) applicable to large undertakings.[^2] It serves as a tool to help SMEs meet data requests from larger business partners, banks, and investors, and to strengthen their own sustainability management.[^3]

---

## Background

### EFRAG and its mandate

EFRAG is an independent organisation whose mission is to serve the European public interest in both financial and sustainability reporting by developing and promoting European positions on corporate reporting.[^4] In the context of sustainability reporting, EFRAG provides technical advice to the European Commission in the form of draft European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), prepared through a rigorous due process, and supports the effective implementation of those standards.[^5] EFRAG seeks input from all stakeholders and collects evidence on specific European circumstances throughout the standard-setting process.[^6]

EFRAG is funded by the European Union through the Single Market Programme, in which the European Economic Area–European Free Trade Association (EEA–EFTA) countries (Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein) and Kosovo participate.[^7] The views and opinions expressed in EFRAG publications reflect solely those of the authors and do not necessarily represent those of the European Union, the European Commission, or participating countries.[^8]

### Regulatory context

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) imposes mandatory sustainability reporting obligations on large undertakings and listed companies in the European Union.[^9] Non-listed micro, small and medium-sized enterprises fall outside the scope of the CSRD; however, they are frequently subject to indirect data requests from large undertakings in their value chains that are themselves subject to CSRD reporting obligations.[^10] The VSME was developed to address this gap by offering a proportionate, voluntary framework that SMEs can adopt to respond to such requests and to communicate sustainability performance to financial institutions and other stakeholders.[^11]

### Size thresholds

Article 3 of Directive 2013/34/EU defines three categories of SMEs based on balance sheet total, net turnover, and average number of employees during the financial year.[^12]

| Category | Balance sheet total | Net turnover | Average employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | ≤ €450,000 | ≤ €900,000 | ≤ 10 |
| Small | ≤ €5 million | ≤ €10 million | ≤ 50 |
| Medium | ≤ €25 million | ≤ €50 million | ≤ 250 |

An enterprise qualifies for a given category if it does not exceed two of the three applicable thresholds.[^13] The VSME also applies to sole traders, enterprises without legal personality, and listed micro-enterprises.[^14]

## Structure of the standard

The VSME standard is organised into two modules that an enterprise may use to prepare its sustainability report.[^15]

### Basic Module

The Basic Module comprises disclosures B1 and B2, which address general information, and basic metrics B3 through B11, covering environmental, social and governance topics.[^16] The Basic Module is the intended approach for micro-enterprises and constitutes the minimum requirement for other enterprises wishing to use the standard.[^17] Application of the Basic Module is a prerequisite for application of the Extended Module.[^18]

The Basic Module is organised as follows:[^19]

**General information**
- *B1 – Accounting policies applied*
- *B2 – Practices, policies and future initiatives for the transition to a more sustainable economy*

**Environmental metrics**
- *B3 – Energy and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions*
- *B4 – Pollution of air, water and soil*
- *B5 – Biodiversity*
- *B6 – Water*
- *B7 – Resource use, circular economy and waste management*

**Social metrics**
- *B8 – Workforce – General characteristics*
- *B9 – Workforce – Health and safety*
- *B10 – Workforce – Remuneration, collective bargaining and training*

**Governance metrics**
- *B11 – Convictions and fines for corruption and bribery*

### Extended Module

The Extended Module provides supplementary disclosures building on B1–B11 and is intended to address the additional information likely to be requested by banks, investors, and business customers beyond what is captured in the Basic Module.[^20]

The Extended Module is organised as follows:[^21]

**General information**
- *C1 – Strategy: business model and sustainability-related initiatives*
- *C2 – Description of practices, policies and future initiatives for the transition to a more sustainable economy*

**Environmental metrics**
- *C3 – GHG reduction targets and climate change*
- *C4 – Climate risks*

**Social metrics**
- *C5 – Additional general workforce characteristics*
- *C6 – Additional own-workforce information – Human rights policies and processes*
- *C7 – Serious adverse human rights incidents*

**Governance metrics**
- *C8 – Revenues from certain sectors and exclusion from EU reference benchmarks*
- *C9 – Gender diversity ratio in the governing body*

If a module is selected, it must be applied in its entirety, subject to the flexibility provisions set out in the standard.[^22] Each disclosure item is, however, required only where it is applicable to the specific circumstances of the enterprise.[^23]

## Principles for preparing the sustainability report

### General requirements

The VSME standard sets out requirements enabling an enterprise to provide relevant information on:[^24]

- how its activities have had, or are likely to have, a positive or negative effect on people or the environment in the short, medium or long term; and
- how environmental and social matters have affected, or are likely to affect, its financial position, performance and cash flows in the short, medium or long term.

### Qualitative characteristics

An enterprise is required to report information that is relevant, faithful, comparable, understandable and verifiable.[^25] Where the enterprise's activities give rise to sustainability issues that are common within its sector or specific to the enterprise itself, additional metrics and narrative information beyond those required by the standard may be included to support these qualitative characteristics.[^26] This includes consideration of Scope 3 GHG emissions as defined in the standard's guidance.[^27]

### Comparative information

An enterprise is required to report comparative information with respect to the preceding year, except for metrics disclosed for the first time. Comparative information is to be reported from the second reporting year onwards.[^28]

### Applicability

Certain disclosures apply only in specific circumstances. Where a disclosure is not applicable to the enterprise's situation, it may be omitted; such an omission implies that the disclosure is not applicable.[^29]

---

## Relationship to other frameworks

The VSME standard was developed with careful regard for consistency with the ESRS applicable to large undertakings, with proportionate requirements defined to reflect the capacity and resources of SMEs.[^30] Unlike the ESRS, the VSME standard carries no legal authority.[^31]

Appendix C of the standard provides background information for financial market participants who are users of the information produced using the standard, including guidance on alignment with other EU regulatory frameworks.[^32]

---

## Appendices

The standard includes three appendices:[^33]

- **Appendix A** – Defined terms: contains definitions of all terms used in the standard. Defined terms appear in bold italics throughout the text.
- **Appendix B** – List of possible sustainability matters: a non-exhaustive reference list of sustainability topics.
- **Appendix C** – Background information for financial market participants using information produced under the standard, including alignment with other EU legislation.
