# Modeling corporate sustainability strategy

Corporate sustainability strategy involves the integration of economic, environmental, and social considerations into a company's strategic planning to ensure long-term competitiveness and value creation. This study presents a model for corporate sustainability that incorporates three strategic domains: market-industry, resource-based, and institutional-based views. The model is supported by empirical analysis of five leading Mexican companies, which demonstrate how sustainable practices can enhance long-term performance and competitive advantage.

## Background

Corporate sustainability has emerged as a critical factor in business strategy, reflecting the need for companies to align their operations with broader societal and environmental goals. The concept is rooted in the broader idea of sustainable development, which emphasizes the balance between economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection. Corporate sustainability is often linked to corporate social responsibility (CSR), which involves the ethical and responsible management of business activities to benefit stakeholders and society at large.

## Modeling corporate sustainability strategy

Corporate sustainability strategy is defined as the possibility to create value through executed strategies that consider economic, environmental, and social restrictions. A company's competitiveness is influenced by its ability to adapt to the constraints imposed by economic, social, and environmental systems. Sustainable companies demonstrate successful long-term performance by developing strategies that sustainably generate and capture value into the future.

The model for corporate sustainability incorporates three strategic domains: market-industry, resource-based, and institutional-based views. These domains are based on the idea that a company's long-term competitiveness depends on its ability to navigate and respond to the constraints imposed by these systems.

### Market-based view

The market-based view of corporate sustainability is rooted in the principles of competitive strategy, which focuses on cost leadership and differentiation. This approach emphasizes the importance of understanding customer preferences and market dynamics to create value. Companies that offer more perceived benefits than their competitors are likely to achieve higher profitability and long-term competitiveness. Sustainable practices, from the perspective of differentiation, enhance firm attributes and achieve differentiation to improve value.

### Resource-based view

The resource-based view of corporate sustainability emphasizes the importance of a company's internal resources and capabilities in achieving competitive advantage. This approach is based on the idea that a company's unique resources and capabilities can provide a sustainable competitive advantage. Sustainable practices are central to a company's business model and survival because a strategy of targeted, enduring actions affords competitive advantages.

### Institutional-based view

The institutional-based view of corporate sustainability focuses on the role of institutional conditions in shaping corporate behavior. This approach emphasizes the importance of aligning corporate strategies with the expectations of stakeholders and the broader societal context. Sustainable practices are influenced by institutional conditions that modify firm behavior and shape the way companies operate.
