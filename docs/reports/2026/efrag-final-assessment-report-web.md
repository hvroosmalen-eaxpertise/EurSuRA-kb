# EFRAG Assessment of Interest in a Voluntary Template for SMEs at the European Single Access Point

The European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG) conducted an assessment of the interest in a voluntary template for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) at the European Single Access Point (ESAP) to facilitate financing. The assessment, based on stakeholder feedback and available literature, concluded that current interest in the template is insufficient to make it a meaningful and impactful tool for SME financing. The report outlines considerations for potential development of the template, including the inclusion of both quantitative and qualitative information, confidentiality concerns, and the need for additional functionalities.

## Background

The European Commission requested EFRAG to evaluate the interest in a KPI-based reporting initiative for SMEs, aiming to improve access to financing. The initiative would be hosted on the ESAP, a platform designed to streamline access to EU financial services. EFRAG's assessment was based on interviews with over 90 stakeholders, survey responses from 39 respondents, six comment letters, and relevant literature. The assessment focused on the potential of the template to address financing challenges, particularly for startups and fast-growing innovative companies.

## Interest from SMEs

SMEs expressed interest in the voluntary template if it could enhance their chances of obtaining financing and if the benefits outweighed the costs and effort of providing information. However, some stakeholders feared that the template could lead to de facto mandatory reporting, which would increase costs. SMEs also raised concerns about the confidentiality of information, noting that the willingness to disclose data varies significantly across jurisdictions.

## Interest from Finance Providers and Other Market Players

Feedback from finance providers and market players was mixed. Most banks, insurance companies, and pension funds did not express interest in using the template. Smaller IPO advisors were interested if they operated across multiple jurisdictions, while domestic-focused advisors were less interested. Public and government financing bodies expressed some interest in using the template for filtering eligible candidates, but noted that they typically receive funding requests rather than actively seeking candidates. Early-stage financing providers, such as business angels and incubators, showed interest only if they were investing internationally. Crowdfunding platforms and some venture capital firms expressed interest in the template for cross-border opportunities and benchmarking. However, larger venture capital firms and private equity funds, which rely on commercial databases and networking, showed limited interest.

## Considerations for a Potential Voluntary Template

EFRAG highlighted that a pan-European database of SME information could serve purposes beyond financing, such as research, benchmarking, and supply chain management. The report suggested that the template should allow for both quantitative and qualitative information, with qualitative data being more relevant at earlier stages of a company's lifecycle. While some stakeholders considered harmonization of financial information beneficial, others did not see a need for it. Confidentiality concerns were emphasized as a key issue, with suggestions to address these through a separate database tailored to SME needs or by extending an existing platform rather than using ESAP.
