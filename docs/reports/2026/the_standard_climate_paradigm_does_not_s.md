--- # Standard Climate Paradigm and the 2D Sheet Approximation The standard climate paradigm does not study the atmosphere in three dimensions. Instead, it models the atmosphere as a two-dimensional sheet, which is used to calculate properties such as opacity and radiative forcing. This approach has been criticized for not accurately representing the physical properties of the real atmosphere, which is a three-dimensional fluid with complex dynamics and interactions. The document under review argues that the standard climate paradigm's methodology leads to a misrepresentation of atmospheric behavior by reducing a three-dimensional system to a two-dimensional projection. ## Background The standard climate paradigm is a framework used to model and predict climate behavior, particularly focusing on the Earth's energy balance and the effects of greenhouse gases. This paradigm relies on a set of assumptions and simplifications to make the complex interactions within the atmosphere computationally tractable. One of the key criticisms of this paradigm is its treatment of the atmosphere as a two-dimensional sheet rather than a three-dimensional fluid. This simplification is attributed to the use of specific tools and methodologies, such as the HITRAN database and the Beer-Lambert law, which are designed for different purposes and may not be appropriate for modeling the full complexity of atmospheric processes. ## The Two Flatteners The document identifies two primary tools or methodologies, referred to as "flatteners," that contribute to the reduction of the atmosphere to a two-dimensional sheet. These flatteners are: 1. **HITRAN (High-Resolution Transmission Molecular Absorption)**: This database is used to model the absorption and emission of radiation by atmospheric gases. HITRAN reduces the three-dimensional properties of molecules to a two-dimensional scalar, which is used in radiative transfer calculations. This reduction eliminates important physical properties such as molecular volume, collisional kinetics, and three-dimensional dissipation, leading to a simplified representation of the atmosphere. 2. **Beer-Lambert Law**: This law is used to describe the attenuation of light as it passes through a medium. When applied to the atmosphere, it integrates the two-dimensional scalars from HITRAN along a one-dimensional ray, effectively projecting the entire column of the atmosphere onto a single transverse plane. This projection eliminates the three-dimensional porosity and other dynamic properties of the real atmosphere, resulting in a two-dimensional sheet that is used to calculate radiative forcing and other climate-related parameters. ## The Cascade Dependency Chain The two flatteners operate in a cascade dependency chain, where the output of one is used as the input for the other. The real atmosphere, which is a three-dimensional fluid with complex dynamics, is first reduced to a two-dimensional scalar by HITRAN. This scalar is then used in the Beer-Lambert law to calculate the optical depth of the atmosphere, resulting in a two-dimensional sheet that is used to model the atmosphere's behavior. This process leads to a significant simplification of the real atmosphere, which may not accurately represent the physical processes occurring within it. ## See Also - [[HITRAN]]
