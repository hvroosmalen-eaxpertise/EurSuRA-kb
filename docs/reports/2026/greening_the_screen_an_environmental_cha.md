--- # Greening the Screen: An Environmental Challenge The motion picture and television industries have increasingly become focal points in discussions about environmental sustainability, as their production processes contribute significantly to carbon emissions and resource consumption. This article explores the environmental impact of film and television production, emphasizing the need for sustainable practices within the industry. By examining the British film and television industry as a case study, the author highlights the challenges and potential for change in embedding environmental sustainability into industrial and economic frameworks. ## Background Environmental sustainability in the motion picture industry has become a subject of debate at multiple levels, particularly within developed film industries where production contributes significantly to energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions. The concept of sustainability, often associated with durable economic development, is increasingly recognized as having a broader meaning that includes the needs of both human and non-human nature. The United Nations World Commission on Environment and Development defined sustainable development in 1987 as "development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." This definition, while anthropocentric, reflects a collective consciousness that separates nature from humankind, a conceptualization that is central to the discussion of environmental sustainability. ## Environmental Impact of the Film and Television Industries Most European countries have initiated campaigns to raise awareness of their carbon footprints, yet comprehensive data on the environmental damage caused by major industries remains limited. The University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) report *Sustainability in the Motion Picture Industry* (2006) is considered the first detailed work on the environmental damage of the film and television industry. The report focuses on the carbon footprint of the motion picture industry in the Los Angeles area and compares it with other industries present in the region. The authors adopt a broad view of sustainability by including measures of human well-being beyond purely environmental ones, using the term "environmental footprint" to consider components such as conventional pollutants, energy use, greenhouse gas emissions, hazardous waste, and fatalities associated with different industries. The motion picture industry is the largest contributor to conventional air pollution within the Los Angeles metropolitan area, creating more pollution than sectors such as aerospace, hotels, apparel, and semiconductor manufacturing. The energy consumption and greenhouse emissions of the motion picture industry also come close to those of the aerospace and apparel industries. The BAFTA Albert Consortium's year three report shows that, in 2014, the average carbon footprint of a television program was 9.4 tonnes per hour of broadcast time. ## Sustainability Practices and Challenges Sustainability practices in the motion picture industry are being developed through the establishment of sustainability departments in film and television companies and the embedding of best practices in film production. These practices are part of responsible and ethical business acts, yet the term "sustainability" is often used primarily to mean durable economic development that prioritizes business benefits, which may not always align with environmental good. The author argues that sustainable development cannot be possible until sustainability is embedded into the overarching industrial and economic regulations.
