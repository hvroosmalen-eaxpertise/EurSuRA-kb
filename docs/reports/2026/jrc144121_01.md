# DigComp 3.0: European Digital Competence Framework

**DigComp 3.0** is the fifth edition of the European Digital Competence Framework (DigComp), published in 2025 by the [[Joint Research Centre]] (JRC) of the European Commission in collaboration with the Directorate-General for Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion (DG EMPL). [^1] The framework describes the knowledge, skills and attitudes required for digital competence across daily life, civic participation, work and learning contexts, and is designed to be technology-neutral and adaptable for use with both children and adults. [^2]

---

## Background

The European Digital Competence Framework was first published in 2013 and has since undergone successive revisions to reflect evolving digital technologies and policy priorities. [^3] DigComp 3.0 constitutes the fifth edition of the framework and incorporates digital technological developments, trends and practices that have occurred since the previous edition in 2022. [^4] The framework serves as the basis for the Digital Skills Indicator (DSI), the instrument used to measure digital skills in the European Union (EU) as part of the [[Digital Decade Policy Programme]]. [^5]

The framework is developed in alignment with major European policy initiatives, including the Union of Skills—in which DigComp is explicitly referenced—the Digital Decade Policy Programme, the AI Continent Action Plan, and the European Strategy for a Better Internet for Kids. [^6] It also integrates key rights and obligations arising from EU digital regulations. [^7]

---

## Framework Components

### Definition of Digital Competence

DigComp 3.0 defines digital competence as encompassing the knowledge, skills and attitudes needed to engage effectively with digital technologies in daily life, society, work and learning environments. [^8] The framework is intended for use by individuals and organisations at local, regional, national, European and international levels. [^9]

### Competence Areas and Competences

The framework organises digital competence into a set of competence areas containing individual competences. These areas address domains including information and data literacy, communication and collaboration, digital content creation, safety and cybersecurity, and problem-solving. [^10]

### Proficiency Levels

DigComp 3.0 incorporates a graduated system of proficiency levels that allow users and organisations to identify and assess the degree of digital competence attained or required. [^11]

### Learning Outcomes

For the first time, DigComp 3.0 introduces a dedicated learning outcomes section, providing a more granular articulation of digital competence. This addition is intended to facilitate consistent interpretation and application of the framework across education, training and employment contexts. [^12]

### Artificial Intelligence Competence

DigComp 3.0 introduces the systematic and transversal integration of [[artificial intelligence]] (AI) competence across the framework. This reflects the widespread diffusion of AI technologies and their implications for digital competence in contemporary society. [^13]

---

## Content Updates

Relative to the previous edition, DigComp 3.0 incorporates substantial updates in the following areas: advancements in AI; cybersecurity; digital rights, choice and responsibility; digital wellbeing; and the challenges posed by increased misinformation. [^14] A linked open data supplement has additionally been produced to enhance interoperability with other machine-readable information, with the stated aim of supporting analysis of digital skills gaps across Europe. [^15]

---

## Development and Consultation

DigComp 3.0 was developed with the expert advice of eleven named specialists and with input from 42 external experts who participated in consultative meetings held in June and October 2024 and in a validation workshop in June 2025. [^16] A stakeholder consultation engaged 260 individuals and organisations from 24 EU Member States, and a further 174 individuals provided feedback during a consultation period in April 2025. [^17]

The publication was authored by J. Cosgrove and R. Cachia of the JRC. [^18]

---

## Adoption and Use

Since its initial publication, DigComp has been widely adopted in employment, education and training initiatives at international, European, national, regional and local levels. [^19] DigComp 3.0 is designed to support implementation across a variety of contexts and to be tailored for specific purposes by public authorities, educational institutions, employers and civil society organisations. [^20]
