# DigComp 3.0: European Digital Competence Framework

**DigComp 3.0** is the fifth edition of the European Digital Competence Framework (DigComp), published in 2025 by the European Commission's [[Joint Research Centre]] (JRC) in collaboration with the Directorate-General for Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion (DG EMPL).[^1] The framework describes the knowledge, skills, and attitudes required for digital competence across contexts including daily life, participation in society, work, and learning.[^1] It incorporates significant updates reflecting advances in artificial intelligence (AI), cybersecurity, digital rights, and misinformation, and introduces, for the first time, a structured learning outcomes approach.[^1]

---

## Background

Since its initial publication in 2013, DigComp has served as a reference framework for digital competence across international, European, national, regional, and local levels.[^2] Its comprehensive scope, scientific robustness, and adaptability have supported widespread adoption in employment, education, and training initiatives.[^2] DigComp also underpins the Digital Skills Indicator (DSI), the instrument used to measure digital skills in the European Union (EU) as part of the Digital Decade Policy Programme.[^2]

DigComp 3.0 aligns with several major European policy initiatives, including the Union of Skills—in which DigComp is explicitly referenced—the Digital Decade Policy Programme, the AI Continent Action Plan, and the European Strategy for a Better Internet for Kids.[^1]

---

## Framework Components

### Definition and Scope

DigComp 3.0 defines [[digital competence]] as the knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed to operate effectively in digital environments.[^1] The framework is technology-neutral and designed to be tailored and adapted for a variety of purposes in education, training, and employment contexts.[^1] It is intended for use with both children and adults, and by individuals as well as local, regional, national, European, and international organisations.[^1]

### Competence Areas and Competences

The framework is organised into competence areas and individual competences that collectively describe the breadth of digital competence required in contemporary society.[^1] These areas address, among other domains, information and data literacy, communication and collaboration, digital content creation, safety, and problem-solving.[^1]

### Proficiency Levels

DigComp 3.0 retains a structured set of proficiency levels that allow users to identify and describe gradations of digital competence, from foundational to highly specialised.[^1]

### Learning Outcomes

A new section outlining learning outcomes is included for the first time in DigComp 3.0.[^1] This well-established policy and practical tool is intended to ensure that the framework is interpreted and applied in an easy and consistent manner.[^2] The learning outcomes provide a more granular view of digital competence and support systematic and transversal integration of AI competence across the framework.[^1]

### AI Competence Integration

DigComp 3.0 integrates AI competence systematically and transversally throughout the framework, reflecting the widespread diffusion of AI technologies and their implications for digital competence.[^1][^2] The update addresses advancements in AI as well as related areas such as cybersecurity, digital rights, choice and responsibility, wellbeing, and the need to address increased misinformation.[^2]

---

## Development Process

DigComp 3.0 was developed by the JRC with the expert advice of eleven named specialists and the participation of 42 experts in initial meetings held in June and October 2024, and in a validation workshop held in June 2025.[^1] A stakeholder consultation engaged 260 individuals and organisations from 24 EU Member States, with a further 174 individuals providing feedback in April 2025.[^1] The framework incorporates digital technological developments, trends, and practices that have occurred since the previous edition published in 2022.[^1]

A linked open data supplement accompanies DigComp 3.0, enhancing its interface with other machine-readable information and offering new avenues to analyse and address digital skills gaps across Europe.[^2]

---

## Policy Context

DigComp 3.0 integrates key rights and obligations under EU digital regulations and is aligned with major European policies addressing the societal and economic implications of digital transformation.[^2] According to the framework's foreword, digital skills are considered critical for ensuring citizen participation in society, learning, work, and social inclusion across Europe.[^2]
