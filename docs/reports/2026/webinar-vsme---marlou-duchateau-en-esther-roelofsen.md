# Voluntary Sustainability Reporting for SMEs: The VSME Standard

The Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (VSME) is a reporting framework published by the [[European Financial Reporting Advisory Group]] (EFRAG) in 2024, designed to enable small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to voluntarily disclose sustainability information in a structured and proportionate manner.[^1] Unlike the mandatory [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive]] (CSRD), which applies to large undertakings, the VSME standard offers a lighter-weight alternative aligned with broader European sustainability policy objectives.[^1] The framework enables SMEs to communicate sustainability performance to value chain partners, financiers, and other stakeholders using a common reporting language.[^1]

---

## Background

The VSME standard emerged from a broader sequence of European and international sustainability policy developments.[^1] Key legislative and regulatory milestones include:

- The **Paris Agreement** (2015), establishing global climate targets;
- The **United Nations Sustainable Development Goals** (UN SDGs, 2015);
- The **Dutch Climate Act** (*Nederlandse Klimaatwet*, 2019);
- The **European Green Deal** (2021);
- The **Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation** (SFDR, 2021);
- The **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive** (CSRD, 2023);
- The **European Sustainability Reporting Standards** (ESRS, 2023); and
- The **Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for SMEs** (VSME, 2024).[^1]

Together, these instruments reflect a systemic effort to embed sustainability considerations into corporate governance, financial markets, and supply chains across Europe.[^1]

---

## Relevance for SMEs in Value Chains

Although the VSME is voluntary for SMEs, sustainability reporting remains practically relevant for smaller enterprises embedded in supply chains of CSRD-obligated companies.[^1] Demand for sustainability data originates from multiple directions within the value chain:

**Upstream chain:** raw material suppliers, semi-finished goods producers, manufacturers, and distributors.[^1]

**Downstream chain:** end users, secondary users, waste processors and recyclers, and distributors.[^1]

Additional stakeholders that may request sustainability information include banks, insurers, certification bodies, and sector associations.[^1] SMEs that supply to large retailers or form part of international chains are therefore indirectly affected by mandatory reporting requirements placed on their partners.[^1]

---

## Strategic Value of Sustainability Reporting

According to CSRD Collectief, a Dutch sustainability advisory organisation, voluntary sustainability reporting using the VSME framework can deliver value across several dimensions:[^1]

- Customer retention and new business development, without risk of [[greenwashing]];
- Attraction and retention of personnel;
- Improved access to capital;
- Development of more sustainable products and services;
- Alignment of organisational mission and vision; and
- Integration of previously disparate sustainability initiatives.[^1]

## The VSME Framework: Structure and Modules

The VSME standard consists of a **basic module** and an **extended module**, which together map onto the thematic structure of the ESRS.[^1] The following table summarises the correspondence between ESRS topics and VSME reporting chapters:

| Sustainability Topic | ESRS Chapter | VSME Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| General information | 2 | B1 + C1 |
| Climate change and energy | E1 | B2 + C2 + B3 + C3 + C4 |
| Pollution (air, water, soil) | E2 | B2 + C2 + B4 |
| Water use and marine resources | E3 | B2 + C2 + B6 |
| Biodiversity and ecosystems | E4 | B2 + C2 + B5 |
| Resource use and circular economy | E5 | B2 + C2 + B7 |
| Own workforce | S1 | B2 + C2 + B8–B10 + C5–C7 + C9 |
| Workers in the value chain | S2 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Affected communities | S3 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Consumers and end users | S4 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Business conduct | G1 | B2 + C2 + B11 + C8 |

[^1]

### Materiality Assessment

The VSME refers to a simplified version of the [[Double Materiality Assessment]] (DMA), which under the full ESRS requires organisations to assess both financial materiality (how sustainability issues affect the enterprise) and impact materiality (how the enterprise affects society and the environment).[^1] For SMEs applying the VSME, this process is proportionately reduced in scope.[^1]

## Implementation: A Six-Step Roadmap

CSRD Collectief presented a practical six-step roadmap for SMEs implementing the VSME framework, illustrated through a fictional case company, *Bordvol*, a tableware manufacturer based in Breda, the Netherlands:[^1]

1. **Determine material topics** – Identify the sustainability themes most relevant to the organisation through a simplified DMA. For Bordvol, these were identified as: climate change, raw materials and circularity, own employees, and business conduct.[^1]

2. **Develop a sustainability strategy** – Formulate a mission and strategic pillars based on the material topics. Bordvol's stated mission was: *"Bordvol puts sustainability on the menu. We are building a chain in which fair materials, smart choices for lower CO₂, and good employment practices together form the standard."*[^1]

3. **Baseline measurement on strategic pillars** – Collect initial data across the selected themes. Bordvol's three pillars were: (i) fair materials, including investigation of the origin of glass and silverware; (ii) smart choices to reduce CO₂, including energy data from the Breda production site; and (iii) good employment, including a code of conduct and payment behaviour review.[^1]

4. **Formulate and implement policies, actions, targets, and KPIs** – For example, Bordvol set a target of achieving climate neutrality by 2035, with total CO₂ emissions in [[Scope 1, 2, and 3]] as the key performance indicator (KPI), and 100% traceability of purchased goods as a target for the raw materials pillar.[^1]

5. **Communicate the sustainability strategy** – Disclosure options range from a single webpage statement (as used by Dutch company Pieter Pot) to an annual sustainability report (as produced by Aviko or BLIJLES), or a combination of web-based communication supplemented by a detailed annual report.[^1]

6. **Systematically improve performance** – Establish a plan-do-check-act cycle to measure and improve sustainability outcomes over time.[^1]

---

## Practical Guidance for Organisations

CSRD Collectief recommends the following steps for SMEs considering voluntary adoption of the VSME standard:[^1]

- Establish an internal working group with the approval of senior management;
- Study the VSME reporting standard as published by EFRAG;
- Work through the six-step roadmap, involving suppliers and customers where possible; and
- Use the framework as a shared language for concrete sustainability progress rather than treating compliance with reporting standards as an end in itself.[^1]
