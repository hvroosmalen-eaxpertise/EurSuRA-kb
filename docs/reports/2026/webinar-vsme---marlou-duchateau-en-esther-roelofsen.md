# Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (VSME): Framework, Application, and Value

The Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (VSME), published by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group ([[EFRAG (European Financial Reporting Advisory Group)]]) in 2024, provide a structured framework enabling smaller organisations to report on sustainability performance on a voluntary basis.[^1] The standard is positioned as an accessible counterpart to the mandatory European Sustainability Reporting Standards ([[ESRS]]) that apply to large undertakings under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive ([[CSRD]]).[^1] According to practitioners at the CSRD Collectief, the VSME is intended not merely as a compliance instrument but as a shared language through which organisations can realise and communicate concrete sustainability progress.[^1]

---

## Background

### Regulatory Context

The VSME sits within a broader sequence of international and European policy developments aimed at embedding sustainability into economic activity.[^1] Key milestones in this regulatory lineage include:

- The **Paris Climate Agreement** (2015), establishing binding temperature targets under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change;[^1]
- The **United Nations Sustainable Development Goals** (SDGs, 2015);[^1]
- The **Dutch Climate Act** (*Nederlandse Klimaatwet*, 2019);[^1]
- The **European Green Deal** (2021);[^1]
- The **Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation** (SFDR, 2021), which imposes sustainability-related disclosure obligations on financial market participants;[^1]
- The **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive** (CSRD, 2023), which extended mandatory sustainability reporting to a broad range of large EU undertakings;[^1]
- The **European Sustainability Reporting Standards** (ESRS, 2023), the detailed technical standards underpinning CSRD reporting;[^1] and
- The **Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for SMEs** (VSME, 2024), developed by EFRAG for entities not subject to mandatory CSRD obligations.[^1]

### Relevance for Non-Mandatory Reporters

Although small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) are generally outside the direct scope of CSRD, sustainability information from such organisations remains relevant to their value chains.[^1] Large CSRD-obligated customers, suppliers, banks, insurers, certifying bodies, and sector associations may request sustainability data from SME partners.[^1] The VSME provides a standardised basis on which such information can be collected and disclosed, reducing ad-hoc data requests and enabling consistent comparisons across supply chains.[^1]

## Structure of the VSME Standard

The VSME comprises a **basic module** and an **extended module**, covering sustainability topics that correspond to the thematic chapters of the ESRS.[^1] The mapping between ESRS topics and VSME chapters is summarised in the table below.

| Sustainability Topic | ESRS Chapter | VSME Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| General information | 2 | B1 + C1 |
| Climate change and energy | E1 | B2 + C2 + B3 + C3 + C4 |
| Pollution (air, water, soil) | E2 | B2 + C2 + B4 |
| Water use and marine resources | E3 | B2 + C2 + B6 |
| Biodiversity and ecosystems | E4 | B2 + C2 + B5 |
| Resource use and circular economy | E5 | B2 + C2 + B7 |
| Own workforce | S1 | B2 + C2 + B8 + B9 + B10 + C5 + C6 + C7 + C9 |
| Workers in the value chain | S2 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Affected communities | S3 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Consumers and end-users | S4 | B2 + C2 + C7 |
| Business conduct | G1 | B2 + C2 + B11 + C8 |

*Source: CSRD Collectief webinar presentation, 8 May 2025.[^1]*

## Relationship to Double Materiality Assessment

A central concept shared by the ESRS and the VSME is **double materiality**, which considers both the financial materiality of sustainability risks to the organisation and the impact materiality of the organisation's activities on people and the environment.[^1] According to the CSRD Collectief, the VSME refers to a simplified version of the Double Materiality Assessment ([[DMA]]) process, making it more accessible for smaller organisations while retaining its analytical logic.[^1] The outcome of a double materiality process determines which sustainability topics are material for a given organisation and therefore require disclosure.

## Implementation: A Six-Step Roadmap

Practitioners presenting at the CSRD Collectief webinar of 8 May 2025 outlined a six-step implementation roadmap applicable to organisations adopting the VSME framework.[^1]

### Step 1: Determining Material Topics

The organisation identifies which sustainability topics are material through a simplified DMA process.[^1] Topics may span environmental themes (e.g., climate change, resource use), social themes (e.g., own workforce, business conduct), and governance considerations.

### Step 2: Developing a Sustainability Strategy

Material topics inform a sustainability mission and overarching strategy.[^1] The strategy provides an organisational context for subsequent measurement and reporting activities.

### Step 3: Baseline Measurement

A baseline (zero measurement) is conducted across each strategic pillar to establish the current state of performance before targets are set.[^1]

### Step 4: Formulating and Implementing Policy, Actions, Targets, and Key Performance Indicators

For each material topic, the organisation formulates policy commitments, specific actions, quantitative targets, and [[Key Performance Indicators]] (KPIs).[^1] These elements are typically recorded in a management dashboard.

### Step 5: Communicating the Sustainability Strategy

Organisations may communicate their sustainability strategy and progress through a range of formats, including website statements, standalone sustainability reports, annual reports, or combined digital and print publications.[^1]

### Step 6: Systematically Improving Performance

Performance against KPIs is monitored and improvement is pursued in a planned manner over successive reporting cycles.[^1]

## Case Illustration: Bordvol

To illustrate the application of the VSME framework, the CSRD Collectief webinar featured a hypothetical case study based on a company named Bordvol, a tableware manufacturer and distributor with a production site in Breda, the Netherlands, supplying customers including large retail chains.[^1] Bordvol had previously begun CSRD preparations but subsequently fell outside the mandatory scope and was considering whether to continue sustainability reporting voluntarily.[^1]

### Material Topics Identified

Following a simplified double materiality process, Bordvol identified four material topics:[^1]

1. Climate change
2. Raw materials and circular economy
3. Own workforce
4. Business conduct

### Sustainability Mission

Based on its material topics, Bordvol formulated the following mission statement: *"Bordvol puts sustainability on the menu. We are building a chain in which fair materials, smart choices for lower CO₂, and good employment practice together form the standard."*[^1]

### Strategic Pillars and Baseline Actions

Bordvol's strategy was structured around three pillars:[^1]

- **Pillar 1 – Fair Materials**: Use of European clay for plates and mugs; provenance of glassware and silverware under investigation.
- **Pillar 2 – Smart Choices for Lower CO₂**: CO₂ data sourced via logistics service provider; energy consumption per production site made transparent.
- **Pillar 3 – Good Employment Practice**: Code of conduct and reporting mechanisms integrated into the staff handbook; payment behaviour under review with an improvement plan in preparation.

### Dashboard Example

A management dashboard aligned with the VSME structure recorded the following for Bordvol's primary pillars:[^1]

| Pillar | Topic | Policy in Place | Actions | Target | KPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fair materials | Raw materials & circularity | No | Develop policy to gain insight into material provenance and recycled content | 100% of purchased goods with known provenance | % of purchased goods with documented provenance |
| Lower CO₂ | Climate change | Yes | Make CO₂ footprint of Breda production site transparent | Climate neutral by 2035 | Total CO₂ emissions – Scope 1, 2, and 3 |
| Good employment | Own workforce & business conduct | Yes | *Further detail to be completed* | *To be defined* | *To be defined* |

## Strategic Value of Voluntary Sustainability Reporting

According to the CSRD Collectief, adopting a sustainability strategy and reporting under the VSME framework can generate value for an SME across multiple dimensions, including:[^1]

- Customer retention and new business development without recourse to unsubstantiated environmental claims ([[greenwashing]]);
- Attraction and retention of employees;
- Improved access to capital from sustainability-oriented financial institutions;
- Development of more sustainable products or services;
- Alignment of organisational mission and vision with operational practice; and
- Integration of previously isolated sustainability initiatives into a coherent strategic framework.
