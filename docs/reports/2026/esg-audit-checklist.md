# ESG Audit Checklist

Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) auditing is a structured process through which organisations assess and verify their performance against non-financial sustainability criteria.[^1] An ESG audit checklist provides auditors and reporting entities with a systematic framework for evaluating disclosures, controls, and outcomes across all three ESG dimensions.[^1] Such checklists have become increasingly relevant following the introduction of mandatory sustainability reporting obligations in multiple jurisdictions.[^1]

## Background

The formalisation of ESG auditing frameworks accelerated in the early 2020s as regulators and standard-setters moved to standardise non-financial reporting.[^1] The [[European Financial Reporting Advisory Group]] (EFRAG), an advisory body that develops technical guidance for sustainability reporting in the European Union, plays a central role in defining the criteria against which ESG audits are conducted.[^1] EFRAG's work underpins the [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards]] (ESRS), which form the basis of disclosure requirements under the [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive]] (CSRD).[^1] An ESG audit checklist aligned with EFRAG guidance therefore reflects the structure and materiality requirements established by the ESRS.[^1]

## Environmental Criteria

The environmental dimension of an ESG audit checklist examines an organisation's impacts on, and dependencies upon, natural systems.[^1] Typical audit items in this category include:

- **Climate change mitigation and adaptation**: verification of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions data across Scope 1 (direct emissions), Scope 2 (indirect emissions from purchased energy), and Scope 3 (value-chain emissions), as well as the existence and credibility of climate transition plans.[^1]
- **Pollution**: assessment of air, water, and soil pollution disclosures, including the identification of substances of concern.[^1]
- **Water and marine resources**: review of water consumption, withdrawal, and recycling disclosures, with particular attention to operations in water-stressed areas.[^1]
- **Biodiversity and ecosystems**: evaluation of impact assessments covering land use, habitat degradation, and alignment with targets such as those established under the [[Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework]].[^1]
- **Resource use and circular economy**: examination of material flows, waste management practices, and progress toward circular economy objectives.[^1]

## Social Criteria

The social dimension addresses an organisation's relationships with its workforce, value-chain workers, affected communities, and consumers.[^1] Key checklist elements include:

- **Own workforce**: verification of data on working conditions, wages, working hours, health and safety performance, and freedom of association.[^1]
- **Workers in the value chain**: assessment of due diligence processes applied to suppliers and subcontractors in line with [[human rights due diligence]] principles.[^1]
- **Affected communities**: review of engagement processes, impact assessments, and remedy mechanisms for communities adjacent to operations.[^1]
- **Consumers and end-users**: examination of product safety, responsible marketing, and data privacy practices.[^1]

## Governance Criteria

The governance dimension evaluates the internal structures and processes through which an organisation exercises accountability and oversight of sustainability matters.[^1] Audit items typically cover:

- **Board composition and oversight**: confirmation that sustainability competencies and responsibilities are embedded at board level.[^1]
- **Business conduct**: review of anti-corruption and anti-bribery policies, whistleblower mechanisms, and political engagement disclosures.[^1]
- **Risk management integration**: assessment of whether ESG risks are incorporated into enterprise risk management (ERM) frameworks.[^1]
- **Incentive structures**: verification that executive remuneration is linked to material sustainability targets.[^1]
- **Lobbying and political contributions**: examination of transparency disclosures regarding advocacy activities.[^1]

## Double Materiality Assessment

A distinctive feature of EFRAG-aligned ESG audit checklists is the requirement to verify completion of a [[double materiality assessment]].[^1] Double materiality requires organisations to evaluate both (1) the financial materiality of ESG factors—how sustainability issues affect the organisation's financial performance—and (2) the impact materiality of ESG factors—how the organisation's activities affect people and the environment.[^1] Auditors are expected to examine the methodology, stakeholder engagement process, and documentation underlying the materiality determination.[^1]

## Assurance and Verification

ESG audit checklists distinguish between internal audit processes and external assurance engagements.[^1] Under the CSRD framework, sustainability statements are subject to mandatory limited assurance, with a phased transition toward reasonable assurance over time.[^1] Checklist items in this section assess whether assurance providers meet independence and competence requirements, and whether assurance opinions are consistent with the disclosures presented.[^1]
