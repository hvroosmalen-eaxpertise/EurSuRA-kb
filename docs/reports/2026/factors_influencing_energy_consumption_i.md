# Factors Influencing Energy Consumption in the Context of Sustainable Development

Energy consumption is a critical factor in the pursuit of sustainable development, with its dynamics influenced by a range of economic, social, and environmental variables. This study explores the relationship between these factors and energy consumption, focusing on primary and final energy use, and highlights the implications for policy and research. The research employs a combination of panel data analysis and bibliometric analysis to provide a comprehensive understanding of the determinants of energy consumption at the European Union level.

## Background

The relationship between energy consumption and economic growth has been a subject of extensive research, with studies indicating both unidirectional and bidirectional causality. For instance, Caraiani et al. [^1] confirmed a causal link between primary energy consumption and gross domestic product (GDP) in several Eastern European countries. Similarly, Asafu-Adjaye [^2] demonstrated that energy consumption is influenced by income variations, suggesting a non-neutral relationship between energy and income. These findings underscore the complexity of energy consumption dynamics and their implications for sustainable development.

## Factors Influencing Energy Consumption

The study identifies several factors that influence energy consumption, with some having a positive relationship and others a negative one. Greenhouse gas emissions, GDP, population growth, and labor growth are found to have a positive relationship with both primary and final energy consumption, indicating an increase in energy use. Conversely, factors such as an increase in the female population, healthcare expenditures, and energy taxes are associated with a reduction in energy consumption. These findings highlight the multifaceted nature of energy consumption and the need for targeted policies to address these influences.

## Methodology and Findings

The research employs a holistic approach, integrating economic, social, and environmental factors to analyze energy consumption at the EU28 level. Data was sourced from Eurostat [^3], the World Bank [^4], and the Energy Information Administration (EIA) [^5]. The study's methodology includes a bibliometric analysis of existing literature and panel data analysis to assess the impact of various factors on energy consumption. The results of these analyses provide insights into the determinants of energy consumption and their implications for sustainable development policies.
