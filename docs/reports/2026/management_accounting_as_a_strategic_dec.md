# Management Accounting as a Strategic Decision-Making Tool

Management accounting plays a critical role in strategic decision-making, particularly in project-oriented family companies, where economic information, field experience, project risk, and family values must be integrated. This article explores how management accounting information is used, interpreted, and integrated in strategic decisions for the growth of PT Patra Anugrah Bakti, a family company engaged in construction and infrastructure services in East Kalimantan, Indonesia. The study highlights the dynamics of family company decision-making and the interplay between formal and informal control mechanisms in strategic processes [^1].

## Background

The evolving business environment, uncertainty in operational costs, and demands for business sustainability have driven companies to adopt information-based strategic decisions. In this context, management accounting has expanded from internal cost tracking to include customer, competitor, market, planning, and performance control data, forming the basis of Strategic Management Accounting (SMA) [^1]. However, the availability of accounting information alone does not determine strategic decisions. Cost, margin, and cash flow figures gain meaning only when contextualized with operational conditions, including field experience, market conditions, resource capacity, and project risk [^1].

In project-oriented companies, the integration of historical data with practical knowledge is essential for assessing risks not fully captured in formal reports. This integration of formal and informal control mechanisms becomes a critical part of the decision-making process. In family businesses, this complexity is further heightened, as decisions are influenced not only by financial goals but also by the owner's authority, reputation, long-term relationships, and business sustainability [^1].

## Management Accounting in Strategic Decision-Making

The study focuses on PT Patra Anugrah Bakti, a family company that has shifted its growth direction from land clearing work to strengthening paving and road maintenance. This strategic shift is reflected in the company's financial performance from 2015 to 2024, indicating a transition from a purely technical adjustment to a strategic decision requiring detailed information on material costs, fuel oil, heavy equipment, labor, mobilization, and project risks [^1].

The research employs an interpretive qualitative approach with an exploratory case study design. Data was collected through semi-structured interviews with four key informants—Owner, Director, Finance/Accounting section, and Operational Manager—alongside non-participant observation and review of internal company documents. The data was analyzed using interpretive thematic analysis, supported by NVivo software, to organize and trace information [^1].

The findings reveal that project cost information, cost budget plans, cost realization, margins, cash flows, and receivables are utilized throughout the project lifecycle. These data are interpreted alongside field experience, market conditions, operational capacity, risks, customer relations, reputation, and business sustainability orientation. This integration underpins decisions related to related diversification, tender pricing, cost control, and project profitability evaluation [^1].

Despite the final authority resting with the Owner, the decision-making process involves cross-functional considerations. Delays in formal information often lead to a temporary reliance on direct communication and practical experience [^1].
