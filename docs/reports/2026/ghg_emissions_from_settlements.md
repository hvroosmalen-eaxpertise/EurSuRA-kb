This article outlines the key parameters and methodologies used to estimate GHG emissions associated with settlement expansion in Sri Lanka, drawing on data and guidelines from various sources. ## Background Settlement expansion in Sri Lanka has contributed to deforestation and associated GHG emissions, primarily through land conversion for urban development, infrastructure, and residential construction. The calculation of these emissions involves estimating the extent of deforestation, the carbon stock of the affected forests, and the resulting emissions from both immediate and ongoing carbon release. These calculations are informed by data from national and international sources, including the Sri Lanka Forest Department, remote sensing studies, and the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) guidelines. ## Key Parameters for Estimating GHG Emissions To estimate GHG emissions from deforestation due to settlements, several key parameters must be considered: 1. **Deforestation Area**: This refers to the total area of forest cleared for settlement development. Data on deforestation rates can be obtained from land use change studies, remote sensing, or government reports. 2. **Forest Type and Carbon Stock**: Different forest types store varying amounts of carbon. Immediate emissions result from direct clearing methods such as slash-and-burn, while ongoing emissions may arise from soil degradation or land conversion to non-forested uses. 4. **Deforestation Methodology**: The method of land conversion (e.g., urban expansion, infrastructure development) influences the rate and extent of deforestation. This can be informed by urban growth data or land use change models. 5. **Soil Carbon Loss**: Deforestation often results in the loss of soil carbon, particularly if the land is converted to agricultural or settlement uses. Soil carbon emission factors vary by soil type and forest management practices. 6. **Land Use Change and Post-Deforestation Land Productivity**: The use of deforested land after conversion to agriculture or settlements affects the carbon footprint. Emissions from agricultural practices or construction activities must be considered. 7. **Timeframe for Emission Estimates**: Defining the time period for deforestation and the release of carbon emissions is essential for accurate estimation. Historical deforestation data and carbon cycle models can provide insights into long-term impacts. ## Example Calculation A simplified example illustrates the process of estimating GHG emissions from deforestation due to settlements: 1. **Estimate the Deforestation Area**: Using satellite imagery or government reports, the area of forest cleared for settlements is determined.
