# Firm Strategy for Sustainability

In the contemporary business landscape, sustainability has become a critical factor for firms seeking to expand their market share through strategic initiatives that project environmental and social responsibility. The evolution of strategic typologies from the early 1960s has traditionally focused on competitive advantage, but the current context of sustainability necessitates a shift towards cooperation and collaboration for collective wellbeing. This paper explores various strategy typologies and rethinks strategy for sustainability, emphasizing the need for implementable and inevitable approaches for firms to achieve this goal [^1].

## Background

The survival and growth of organizations depend on strategic decisions made by top management, which shape the long-term future of the firm in terms of economic, societal, and environmental benefits. In the pursuit of competitive advantage, firms often engage in over consumption and over production, which can be detrimental to sustainability performance. This paper argues that cooperation, rather than competition, is essential for sustainable development, as it fosters the welfare of society and the environment. Firms have increasingly focused on sustainability, but the effectiveness of their efforts remains a subject of debate. The paper highlights the importance of grounded, action-driven strategies that reflect genuine commitment to sustainability rather than mere greenwashing [^1].

## Sustainability Strategy

The foundation of a sustainable future lies in changing consumption patterns through innovation. Firms pursuing sustainability strategies must influence and improve sustainable consumption behavior. A firm that performs sustainably achieves profits for shareholders while implementing business practices that enhance its relationship with the natural world and human society. Sustainable development for enterprises involves taking care of human factors, reducing adverse impacts on nature, respecting human values, adopting a holistic systemic view, and creating durable products and services to avoid overproduction and overconsumption [^1].

Strategic leadership plays a crucial role in driving sustainability initiatives, supported by infrastructure and policies. External efforts can only bring firms to a reporting arena, where they present actions in numerical terms, regardless of their real intent. Therefore, sustainability performance is an internal factor of the organization, embedded into the firm strategy driven by top management. Sustainability strategy must incorporate knowledge management practices to bring about changes in products, processes, and markets. It must also be dynamic, allowing firms to adapt to technological changes and foster better relationships with stakeholders to achieve the triple bottom line [^1].

Many firms perceive investments in social and environmental factors as a trade-off for financial performance, but empirical evidence suggests that firms can implement environmental, social, and governance strategies without significant financial costs. Green practices can reduce process costs and wastages, and green supply chain management has been shown to improve firm performance. Transparent reporting of sustainability efforts builds trust with stakeholders, while a strong corporate culture with social responsibility and environmental concern can infuse value into employees at all levels [^1].

Social dilemmas often arise between short-term self-interest and long-term collective interests. Firms may prioritize short-term gains over long-term benefits, leading to exploitative practices. Cooperation, love, and sacrifice are essential for long-term well-being. Game theory has demonstrated that cooperation leads to welfare for all stakeholders. Integrating economic and ethical considerations into strategic models can coexist through an integral approach, fostering a strong value system that enhances employee satisfaction and drives sustainable competitiveness [^1].
