# Building a Sustainable Future: ESG Business Handbook

## Environmental, Social and Governance Standards for Business in Bosnia and Herzegovina

The *Building a Sustainable Future: ESG Business Handbook* is a guidance document produced by the Advice on Individual Rights in Europe (AIRE) Centre and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH), first published in 2023.[^1] The handbook introduces Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) standards and the United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) to businesses operating in BiH, with particular emphasis on small and medium enterprises (SMEs).[^1] It provides practical guidance on implementing ESG frameworks, conducting materiality assessments, and aligning business strategy with internationally recognised sustainability standards.[^1] The document was produced with financial support from UNDP Bosnia and Herzegovina and is intended for businesses of all sizes and sectors.[^1]

---

## Background

### Publication and Authorship

The handbook was produced as part of the AIRE Centre's *ESG Standards – Introduction for Businesses* project.[^1] It was edited by Biljana Braithwaite, Western Balkans Programme Director at the AIRE Centre and Founding Partner of Sustineri Partners, and Sabina Đapo, Western Balkans Programme Manager at the AIRE Centre.[^1] Principal authors are Nevena Kostić, ESG Expert at Sustineri Partners, and Amina Hujdur, Communications Specialist for the AIRE Centre's Western Balkans Programme.[^1] Additional contributions were made by Elma Veledar Arifagić (Attorney at Law, dmb partners Sarajevo), Aleksandra Njagulj (Founding Partner, Sustineri Partners), and Maša Njegovan (ESG and Sustainability Leader, Risk Advisory, Deloitte Advisory d.o.o.).[^1] Case study material was provided by Kakanj cement plant, Smrčak, ETMAX, and Klika.[^1]

### Context and Purpose

As the handbook notes, businesses are increasingly expected not only to achieve financial performance but also to "do good" and "do no harm" in relation to social, environmental, and governance concerns.[^1] Investor and consumer behaviour has shifted toward prioritising companies whose practices align with broader societal values, making ESG adoption a strategic business consideration.[^1] The handbook situates these developments within the context of global challenges including [[climate change]], biodiversity loss, inequality, and corruption.[^1]

## Definition and Components of ESG Standards

ESG standards evaluate a business's sustainability and impact across environmental, social, and governance dimensions, extending beyond financial performance metrics.[^1] Some ESG requirements are imposed by national laws and regulations, while others reflect stakeholder expectations and investor pressure arising from concerns about human rights and environmental issues.[^1]

### Environmental Standards

Environmental standards address a business's impact on the natural environment through its consumption of energy and raw materials.[^1] Relevant factors include contributions to [[climate change]], pollution, waste generation, and natural resource depletion.[^1] Business strategies to reduce negative environmental impact may include targets to reduce [[greenhouse gas]] (GHG) emissions, invest in [[renewable energy]], and promote the use of sustainable resources.[^1] The handbook also notes that businesses themselves are affected by environmental conditions, including floods, droughts, and biodiversity loss.[^1]

### Social Standards

Social standards concern the impact businesses make on society, encompassing labour and human rights, inclusion, equality, and community development.[^1] Positive social impacts can be demonstrated through workplace diversity and inclusion initiatives, fair labour practices throughout supply chains, and active engagement with local communities.[^1]

### Governance Standards

Governance standards address the practices and procedures adopted by a business in its internal management and oversight structures.[^1] These include matters of corporate accountability, transparency, anti-corruption measures, and board composition.[^1]

## Evolution of ESG Standards

### From Corporate Social Responsibility to ESG

The handbook traces the development of ESG standards from earlier [[Corporate Social Responsibility]] (CSR) frameworks, characterising the transition as a move from voluntary action to regulated obligation.[^1] CSR initiatives were largely discretionary; ESG standards, by contrast, are increasingly embedded in binding legal and regulatory frameworks at both national and supranational levels.[^1]

### ESG Investing

The handbook identifies ESG investing as a catalyst for sustainable transition, noting that capital allocation decisions by institutional investors have contributed to the mainstreaming of ESG criteria in business strategy and reporting.[^1]

## International Frameworks and Sustainable Development Goals

The handbook outlines several international frameworks relevant to ESG implementation in BiH.

### UN Global Compact

The [[UN Global Compact]] (UNGC) is a voluntary initiative that calls on businesses to align their operations with ten principles covering human rights, labour, environment, and anti-corruption.[^1]

### Principles for Responsible Investment

The [[Principles for Responsible Investment]] (PRI) is a UN-supported network that encourages investors to incorporate ESG factors into their investment decision-making processes.[^1]

### Sustainable Development Goals

The [[Sustainable Development Goals]] (SDGs) are a set of 17 global goals adopted by the UN in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.[^1] The handbook identifies ESG standards as a mechanism through which the business sector can contribute more robustly to SDG implementation.[^1]

### UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights

The [[UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights]] (UNGPs) provide a framework for states and businesses to prevent and address adverse human rights impacts linked to business activity.[^1]

### Paris Agreement

The [[Paris Agreement]] is an international treaty on climate change, adopted in 2015, which sets targets for limiting global average temperature increases and to which BiH is a signatory.[^1] The handbook presents the agreement as a key reference point for the environmental dimension of ESG strategy.[^1]

## EU Regulatory Framework

The handbook provides an overview of European Union (EU) instruments relevant to ESG, reflecting BiH's status as a candidate for EU accession.

### Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive

The EU [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive]] (CSRD) establishes mandatory sustainability reporting requirements for large companies and listed entities operating within the EU, replacing the earlier Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD).[^1] The European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG) develops the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) under the CSRD framework.[^1]

### EU Taxonomy and Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation

The [[EU Taxonomy Regulation]] is a classification system that defines which economic activities can be considered environmentally sustainable.[^1] The [[Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation]] (SFDR) requires financial market participants to disclose how they integrate ESG risks and sustainability factors into their investment decisions.[^1]

### Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive

The EU [[Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive]] (CSDDD, also referred to as CSDD) requires companies to identify and address actual and potential adverse impacts on human rights and the environment across their value chains.[^1]

### Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism

The EU [[Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism]] (CBAM) is a policy instrument that places a carbon price on imports of certain goods from countries with less stringent climate policies, aiming to prevent [[carbon leakage]].[^1] The handbook notes the mechanism's relevance for BiH exporters supplying EU markets.[^1]

### International Sustainability Standards Board

The [[International Sustainability Standards Board]] (ISSB), established under the IFRS Foundation, develops global baseline sustainability disclosure standards intended to provide investors with consistent, comparable sustainability-related financial information.[^1]

## ESG Standards in the BiH Regulatory Framework

The handbook provides an overview of the existing domestic legal framework in BiH as it relates to ESG obligations, noting that domestic legislation is in a transitional phase relative to EU standards.[^1] The contribution of Elma Veledar Arifagić addresses the current state of ESG-related provisions within BiH law.[^1]

## Getting Started with ESG: Practical Guidance

The handbook outlines a structured approach for businesses beginning an ESG programme, covering:

- **Materiality assessment**: identifying ESG issues most relevant to the business and its stakeholders.[^1]
- **Gap analysis**: evaluating the difference between current practices and ESG standards or targets.[^1]
- **Defining ESG goals and indicators**: establishing measurable objectives aligned with recognised frameworks.[^1]
- **Formulating an ESG strategy**: developing a coherent plan that integrates ESG considerations into core business operations.[^1]
- **Recognising risks and opportunities**: understanding how ESG factors affect business risk exposure and competitive positioning.[^1]

### Overview of Reporting Frameworks

The handbook reviews major ESG reporting frameworks available to businesses, enabling organisations to select the approach most appropriate to their sector, size, and stakeholder requirements.[^1]

## Case Studies: UNDP SDG Business Pioneers Award Winners

The handbook profiles four BiH companies recognised as past winners of the UNDP SDG Business Pioneers Award, illustrating practical ESG implementation.

### Kakanj Cement

Kakanj cement plant is presented as an example of sustainability transformation within an energy-intensive industry, demonstrating how a cement manufacturer can adopt ESG-aligned practices to reduce environmental impact.[^1]

### Smrčak

Smrčak is described as a business that empowers communities through sustainable practices, illustrating the social dimension of ESG implementation at a local level.[^1]

### ETMAX

ETMAX is characterised as a pioneer in renewable energy transition in BiH, providing a case study of a company contributing to the environmental pillar of ESG through clean energy solutions.[^1]

### Klika

Klika is presented as representing a new era of sustainability in BiH's business landscape, with practices reflecting integrated ESG thinking.[^1]

## Recommendations

The handbook concludes with recommendations organised under two headings.

### ESG Strategy

Businesses are advised to develop a formal ESG strategy that is integrated into overall corporate strategy, supported by senior leadership, and aligned with internationally recognised frameworks such as the SDGs and ESRS.[^1]

### Reporting

Businesses are encouraged to adopt transparent ESG reporting practices using established frameworks, to ensure consistent disclosure to stakeholders and to prepare for anticipated regulatory requirements as BiH progresses toward EU alignment.[^1]
