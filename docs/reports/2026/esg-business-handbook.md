# ESG Business Handbook: Environmental, Social and Governance Standards for Businesses in Bosnia and Herzegovina

The *Building a Sustainable Future: ESG Business Handbook* is a practical guidance document produced by the Advice on Individual Rights in Europe (AIRE) Centre and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH), first published in 2023. [^1] The handbook introduces Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) standards and United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) to businesses operating in BiH, with particular emphasis on small and medium enterprises (SMEs). [^2] It combines an overview of international regulatory frameworks, European Union (EU) instruments, and domestic legislation with case studies drawn from past recipients of the UNDP SDG Business Pioneers Award in BiH. [^3]

---

## Background

The handbook was produced under the AIRE Centre's *ESG Standards – Introduction for Businesses* project, financially supported by UNDP Bosnia and Herzegovina. [^1] It was edited by Biljana Braithwaite (Western Balkans Programme Director, AIRE Centre / Founding Partner, Sustineri Partners) and Sabina Đapo (Western Balkans Programme Manager, AIRE Centre), and authored by Nevena Kostić (ESG Expert, Sustineri Partners) and Amina Hujdur (Communications Specialist, AIRE Centre Western Balkans Programme). [^1] Additional contributions were made by Elma Veledar Arifagić (Attorney at Law, dmb partners Sarajevo), Aleksandra Njagulj (Founding Partner, Sustineri Partners), and Maša Njegovan (ESG and Sustainability Leader, Deloitte Advisory d.o.o.). [^1]

The document is directed at businesses of all sizes and sectors in BiH, with a stated focus on SMEs, and is intended to enable organisations to implement ESG standards, assess performance, and define goals and action plans contributing to sustainable development. [^2]

## ESG Standards: Definition and Components

ESG standards evaluate a business's sustainability and its impact on environmental, social, and governance issues beyond financial performance. [^3] According to the handbook, some standards are imposed by national laws and regulations, while others arise from stakeholder expectations and investor pressure linked to growing concerns regarding human rights and environmental degradation. [^3]

### Environmental Standards

Environmental standards address a business's impact on the natural environment through its consumption of energy and raw materials. [^3] Relevant factors include contributions to [[climate change]], pollution, waste generation, and natural resource depletion. [^3] Strategies to reduce negative environmental impact may include setting targets to reduce [[greenhouse gas]] (GHG) emissions, investing in renewable energy, and promoting the use of sustainable resources. [^3] The handbook also notes that businesses are themselves affected by environmental conditions, including floods, droughts, and biodiversity loss. [^3]

### Social Standards

Social standards concern a business's impact on society, encompassing labour and human rights, inclusion, equality, and community development. [^3] Positive social impacts cited in the handbook include promoting workplace diversity and inclusion, ensuring fair labour practices throughout supply chains, and engaging with local communities. [^3]

### Governance Standards

Governance standards relate to the practices and procedures adopted by a business in its internal management, oversight, and decision-making processes. [^3]

## Evolution of ESG Standards

The handbook traces the development of ESG standards from earlier frameworks of [[corporate social responsibility]] (CSR), characterising the transition as a progression from voluntary action to regulated obligation. [^2] Key historical milestones are examined alongside a survey of UN conventions and instruments relating to sustainability. [^2]

---

## International Frameworks

The handbook outlines several international frameworks relevant to businesses in BiH:

- **UN Global Compact (UNGC):** A voluntary corporate sustainability initiative aligning business strategies with universal principles on human rights, labour, environment, and anti-corruption. [^2]
- **Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI):** An investor-led framework promoting the incorporation of ESG factors into investment analysis and decision-making. [^2]
- **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** A set of 17 goals adopted by UN member states in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. [^2]
- **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs):** A framework establishing the responsibilities of states and businesses with respect to human rights. [^2]
- **Paris Agreement:** An international treaty on climate change adopted in 2015, setting targets for limiting global average temperature increases. [^2]

---

## EU Regulatory Framework

The handbook provides an overview of EU instruments that directly affect or are relevant to businesses in BiH, given the country's aspirations toward EU integration:

- **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD):** An EU directive requiring large companies and listed entities to report on sustainability matters in accordance with European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) developed by the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG). [^2]
- **EU Taxonomy and Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR):** Regulatory instruments forming part of the EU's [[sustainable finance]] framework, establishing criteria for classifying environmentally sustainable economic activities. [^2]
- **Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD):** An EU directive requiring companies to identify, prevent, mitigate, and account for adverse human rights and environmental impacts in their operations and supply chains. [^2]
- **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM):** An EU policy instrument placing a carbon price on imports of certain goods from outside the EU to prevent [[carbon leakage]]. [^2]
- **International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB):** An international standard-setting body whose standards are noted in the handbook as relevant to global ESG disclosure. [^2]

---

## ESG Standards in Bosnia and Herzegovina

The handbook surveys the current state of ESG-related legislation and practice in BiH, situating domestic developments within the broader international and EU context. [^2] It further provides practical recommendations covering materiality assessment, gap analysis, definition of ESG goals and indicators, formulation of ESG strategy, and identification of risks and opportunities. [^2]

---

## ESG Investing

The handbook characterises ESG investing as a catalyst for sustainable transition, noting that investor and consumer behaviour increasingly favours companies whose practices align with sustainability values. [^3] This trend is identified as a structural driver encouraging businesses to adopt ESG frameworks irrespective of regulatory requirements. [^3]

---

## Case Studies

The handbook presents case studies based on past winners of the UNDP SDG Business Pioneers Award in BiH, illustrating how ESG principles have been applied in diverse sectoral contexts:

- **Kakanj Cement Plant:** Described as an example of sustainability transformation within the cement manufacturing industry. [^2]
- **Smrčak:** Cited for community empowerment through sustainable business practices. [^2]
- **ETMAX:** Highlighted for its role in advancing renewable energy transition in BiH. [^2]
- **Klika:** Presented as a case study in contemporary sustainability practice. [^2]
