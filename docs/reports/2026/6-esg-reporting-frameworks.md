# Sustainability and ESG Reporting Frameworks

**Sustainability and [[ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance)]] (Environmental, Social, and Governance) reporting frameworks** are structured standards and guidelines that organisations use to disclose their environmental, social, and governance performance to investors, regulators, and other stakeholders. Six frameworks have gained particular prominence globally: the [[GRI]] (Global Reporting Initiative), [[ESRS]] (European Sustainability Reporting Standards), [[TCFD]] (Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures), [[SASB]] (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board), [[ISSB]] (International Sustainability Standards Board), and [[CDP]] (Carbon Disclosure Project). These frameworks vary in scope, mandatory applicability, and thematic focus, reflecting the growing regulatory and investor demand for standardised, comparable sustainability data. [^1]

---

## Background

Growing global pressure for corporate transparency has accelerated the development of sustainability reporting standards. Investors increasingly demand [[material]] ESG data to inform capital allocation decisions, while regulators in multiple jurisdictions have introduced or strengthened mandatory disclosure requirements. [^1] A key concern driving standardisation efforts is the prevention of [[greenwashing]]—the practice of making misleading or unsubstantiated claims about environmental performance—and the promotion of corporate accountability. [^1] The proliferation of frameworks has prompted convergence efforts, most notably through the consolidation of TCFD and SASB into the ISSB. [^1]

## Framework Descriptions

### 1. Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)

The **Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)** is described as the most widely used sustainability reporting framework worldwide. [^1] First published in 1997, GRI provides standards enabling organisations to disclose how their activities affect people and the planet across economic, environmental, and social themes. [^1] The GRI Standards 2021 are currently in effect. [^1] Adoption of GRI is voluntary; however, it has achieved broad uptake among organisations seeking general accountability to a wide range of stakeholders. [^1]

### 2. European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)

The **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)** constitute the mandatory disclosure framework developed under the EU's [[CSRD]] (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive). [^1] ESRS covers the full ESG scope—social, governance, and climate—and applies to large and listed companies operating within the European Union. [^1] Mandatory reporting under ESRS took effect from January 2024. [^1] A defining feature of ESRS is its requirement for **[[double materiality]]** reporting, under which companies must disclose both financial materiality (how sustainability issues affect the company's financial position) and impact materiality (how the company's activities affect people and the environment). [^1]

### 3. Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)

The **Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)** was launched in 2017 to help organisations disclose climate-related financial risks in a consistent and comparable manner. [^1] The framework is structured around four thematic pillars: Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, and Metrics and Targets. [^1] Mandatory TCFD-aligned reporting applies to listed companies in several jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom, Japan, and New Zealand. [^1] TCFD subsequently served as a foundational basis for the standards developed by the ISSB. [^1]

### 4. Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB)

The **Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB)** provides sector-specific guidance designed to help companies disclose financially material ESG information relevant to investors. [^1] SASB standards, developed from 2018, cover 77 industries and focus on industry-specific financial materiality rather than broad stakeholder impact. [^1] SASB was subsequently merged into the ISSB, with its industry-specific guidance incorporated into the ISSB standards framework. [^1] Primary users include public companies and investor-focused ESG disclosure processes. [^1]

### 5. International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)

The **International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)**, established under the [[IFRS]] (International Financial Reporting Standards) Foundation, aims to harmonise ESG reporting globally by providing a baseline standard for sustainability and climate disclosures. [^1] The ISSB integrates the approaches of both TCFD and SASB. [^1] Two standards are currently in effect from January 2024: **IFRS S1**, covering general sustainability-related financial disclosures, and **IFRS S2**, addressing climate-related disclosures. [^1] The ISSB standards target multinational companies and are gaining adoption across multiple jurisdictions. [^1]

### 6. Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP)

The **Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP)** is a global disclosure platform active since 2000 that collects self-reported environmental data from companies to measure climate-related risks and progress. [^1] CDP focuses on three environmental themes: climate change, water security, and deforestation. [^1] Disclosure through CDP is typically triggered by requests from investors or governments. [^1] CDP's methodology is aligned with TCFD recommendations and [[science-based targets]]. [^1] In 2023, more than 18,000 companies disclosed environmental data through the CDP platform. [^1]

## Selecting an Appropriate Framework

The source document identifies distinct use cases for each framework.[^1] GRI is recommended for organisations prioritising broad impact assessment and multi-stakeholder engagement. [^1] SASB, TCFD, and ISSB are identified as particularly suited to financial disclosures intended for investor audiences. [^1] CDP is recommended where climate and environmental transparency is the primary objective. [^1] ESRS applies specifically to organisations subject to EU regulatory compliance obligations under the CSRD. [^1]
