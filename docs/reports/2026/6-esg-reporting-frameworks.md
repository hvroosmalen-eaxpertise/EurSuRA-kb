# Sustainability and ESG Reporting Frameworks

Sustainability and [[ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance)]] reporting frameworks are standardised systems that guide organisations in disclosing their environmental, social, and governance performance and impacts. Six frameworks have achieved widespread institutional recognition: the [[Global Reporting Initiative]] (GRI), the [[European Sustainability Reporting Standards]] (ESRS), the [[Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures]] (TCFD), the [[Sustainability Accounting Standards Board]] (SASB), the [[International Sustainability Standards Board]] (ISSB), and the [[Carbon Disclosure Project]] (CDP).[^1] Increasing regulatory pressure, investor demand for [[material]] ESG data, and the imperative to prevent [[greenwashing]] have collectively elevated the importance of structured sustainability disclosure.[^1]

---

## Background

Growing global concern over climate change, social equity, and corporate governance has driven regulators, investors, and civil society to demand greater transparency from organisations regarding their non-financial impacts.[^1] Sustainability reporting provides a mechanism by which companies communicate how their operations affect people, the environment, and financial stakeholders. The proliferation of frameworks since the late 1990s reflects diverse stakeholder needs, ranging from broad accountability to jurisdiction-specific regulatory compliance and investor-grade financial materiality.[^1] Efforts to harmonise these frameworks have accelerated in the 2020s, notably through the establishment of the ISSB and the mandating of ESRS under European Union law.[^1]

## Major Frameworks

### Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)

The Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) is the most widely adopted sustainability reporting framework in the world.[^1] First published in 1997, GRI enables organisations to disclose how their activities affect people and the planet across economic, environmental, and social dimensions.[^1] The GRI Standards 2021, the current operative version, are voluntary in nature but have achieved broad adoption across sectors and geographies.[^1] GRI is particularly suited to organisations seeking comprehensive stakeholder accountability rather than exclusively investor-focused disclosure.[^1]

### European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)

The European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) are a set of mandatory reporting standards developed under the European Union's [[Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive]] (CSRD).[^1] Applicable to large and listed companies operating in the EU, the ESRS became mandatory from January 2024.[^1] A defining feature of ESRS is the requirement for [[double materiality]] reporting, under which companies must disclose both financial materiality — how sustainability risks and opportunities affect the company's financial position — and impact materiality — how the company's activities affect society and the environment.[^1]

### Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)

The Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) was launched in 2017 to help organisations disclose climate-related financial risks in a consistent and comparable manner.[^1] The framework is structured around four thematic pillars: Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, and Metrics and Targets.[^1] Disclosure aligned with TCFD recommendations has become mandatory for listed companies in several jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom, Japan, and New Zealand.[^1] The TCFD framework subsequently served as a foundational basis for the standards developed by the ISSB.[^1]

### Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB)

The Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB) provides industry-specific guidance designed to assist companies in disclosing financially material ESG information relevant to investors.[^1] Developed in 2018, SASB standards cover 77 distinct industries, enabling sector-appropriate disclosure of ESG factors that are most likely to affect financial performance.[^1] SASB has since been consolidated into the ISSB, which now maintains and develops its standards.[^1]

### International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)

The International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) was established to create a global baseline for ESG and climate-related disclosures, drawing on both the TCFD framework and the SASB standards.[^1] The ISSB operates under the [[IFRS Foundation]] and has published two initial standards: IFRS S1, which addresses general sustainability-related financial disclosures, and IFRS S2, which addresses climate-related disclosures.[^1] Both standards took effect from January 2024 and are gaining adoption across multiple jurisdictions as multinational companies seek a harmonised reporting baseline.[^1]

### Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP)

The Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) is a not-for-profit organisation that collects self-reported environmental data from companies, cities, and other entities to measure climate-related risks and progress toward environmental targets.[^1] Active since 2000, the CDP focuses on three thematic areas: climate change, water security, and deforestation.[^1] Disclosure is typically requested by investors and governments, and the CDP's methodology is aligned with the TCFD framework and [[science-based targets]].[^1] More than 18,000 companies disclosed data through the CDP in 2023.[^1]

---

## Framework Selection

The choice of reporting framework depends on an organisation's primary disclosure objectives, stakeholder audience, and applicable regulatory requirements.[^1] GRI is generally suited to organisations prioritising broad impact reporting and multi-stakeholder accountability.[^1] SASB, TCFD, and ISSB are oriented toward investor-grade financial disclosure and the communication of material ESG risks and opportunities to capital markets.[^1] CDP serves organisations seeking to demonstrate environmental transparency, particularly on climate, water, and deforestation metrics.[^1] ESRS is the applicable standard for organisations subject to EU regulatory compliance obligations under the CSRD.[^1]
