# Voluntary Sustainability Reporting for SMEs: VSME Standard Implementation Guidance

The Voluntary Sustainability Reporting for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (VSME) standard is a framework developed under the auspices of the European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG) to enable smaller organisations to report on sustainability matters voluntarily and in a structured manner. A webinar hosted by the CSRD Collectief on 8 May 2025 addressed practical questions from small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) regarding tooling, implementation approaches, and regulatory developments related to the VSME standard.[^1]

---

## Background

The VSME standard is distinct from the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), which apply to larger organisations subject to the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). Whereas the ESRS impose mandatory disclosure requirements, the VSME is designed as a separate, voluntary standard intended specifically for smaller enterprises that fall outside the scope of CSRD obligations.[^1] The standard aims to facilitate transparency rather than prescribe technical perfection, allowing organisations to begin reporting without significant investment in specialist software.[^1]

---

## Tooling and Data Registration

### General Guidance

According to presenters at the May 2025 webinar, organisations are not required to adopt dedicated or expensive software to begin reporting under the VSME framework. The standard imposes no requirements regarding tooling; the primary objective is transparency.[^1] Many organisations initiate the process using spreadsheet software such as Microsoft Excel, which the presenters describe as a low-threshold method suitable for identifying initial data needs and material themes within an organisation. Consolidating an organisation's policies, actions, objectives, and key performance indicators (KPIs) in a single location simplifies any future transition to specialist software.[^1]

Organisations are advised first to assess existing systems, including:

- Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems, some of which are developing CSRD/VSME extensions
- Quality or environmental management tools
- Existing systems used for KPI measurement and monitoring[^1]

### EFRAG-Provided Tool

EFRAG has published a VSME tool in the form of an Excel document that can be configured into an eXtensible Business Reporting Language (XBRL)-tagged document, available via the EFRAG website.[^1] However, as of the date of the webinar, the CSRD Collectief did not recommend the tool for immediate use, citing errors encountered during initial testing when dates were entered into the template. The Collectief reported that it had contacted EFRAG regarding this issue.[^1]

### Third-Party Tools

Practitioners associated with the CSRD Collectief reported experience with several third-party tools:

- **ESG4MKB** ([esg-for-sme.eu](https://esg-for-sme.eu/)): described as low-threshold and affordable at approximately €90 per year; the presenter noted active involvement in adapting this tool to the VSME structure.[^1]
- **SmartTrackers** ([smarttrackers.nl](https://www.smarttrackers.nl/)): described as more suitable for larger organisations with substantial existing data monitoring infrastructure, offering flexibility and integration with existing processes.[^1]
- **BouwInfosys ERP modules** (BouwWorks, InstalWorks, and InfraWorks): a VSME module was under development at the time of the webinar and was expected to be available in a forthcoming release version.[^1]

Additional tools referenced include Coolset, Greenly, and ResultX, though no detailed assessments of these were provided.[^1]

### Data Collection Planning

For organisations not yet ready to integrate multiple systems, the webinar recommended the use of a data collection plan — a document recording which data are collected, their sources, and the responsible parties. This approach enables consistent repetition of the data collection process in subsequent reporting periods. An example structure is as follows:[^1]

| Theme | What is measured | Source | Frequency | Responsible party |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy consumption | kWh consumption at production site | Energy supplier / smart meter | Monthly | Technical manager |
| Transport | CO₂ emissions per shipment | Logistics service provider / transport invoice | Quarterly | Logistics / administration |
| Employees | Number of employees (FTE) | HR system / personnel administration | Monthly | HR |
| Code of conduct | Number of reports of unsafe situations | Reporting system / personnel reports | Annually | HR / management |
| Materials use | Percentage of products with European-sourced materials | Procurement data / supplier information | Quarterly | Procurement |

## Approach and Implementation

### Basic Module versus Extended Module

The webinar presenters advised that the most important consideration for SMEs is to begin reporting, irrespective of whether they commence with only the basic module or with both the basic and extended modules. The basic module is described as a sound starting point that supports prioritisation.[^1] Organisations may add the extended module when greater depth is required or when chain partners (supply chain partners) request more detailed information.[^1]

One case noted during the webinar involved an organisation that received detailed requests from chain partners specifically regarding CO₂ data. That organisation chose to supplement its VSME reporting by consulting the relevant ESRS standard, specifically ESRS E1, which addresses climate change.[^1] The presenters emphasised that initiating action takes precedence over achieving completeness or perfection from the outset.[^1]

### Example Reports

At the time of the webinar, EFRAG was expected to publish a number of example reports during 2025.[^1] In the interim, fictional examples — including a case study referred to as "Bordvol" presented during the webinar — were available. The CSRD Collectief indicated it was developing additional practical examples for future publication.[^1]

---

## Regulatory Developments

### Dutch-Language Version

As of May 2025, only the English-language version of the VSME standard carried official status. Formal approval of the standard by the European Commission was expected in June 2025, after which translations into other European languages were anticipated.[^1] The CSRD Collectief produced an unofficial Dutch-language translation using artificial intelligence (AI) assistance, which it made available to participants with an explicit caveat that the English-language version must always be used as the authoritative reference for reporting purposes.[^1]

### Impact of the Omnibus Proposal on the VSME

In response to the European Commission's Omnibus proposal — a regulatory initiative aimed at simplifying the ESRS — EFRAG was engaged in a process of revising and simplifying the ESRS at the time of the webinar. The presenters indicated no direct evidence that EFRAG intended to revise the VSME texts as a result.[^1] However, an indirect effect was identified: if the ESRS are substantially simplified, the gap between the ESRS and the VSME may narrow, potentially creating pressure for adjustments to the VSME in due course. The presenters nonetheless affirmed that the VSME, as a distinct voluntary standard for smaller businesses, would remain relevant for organisations not subject to CSRD obligations.[^1]
