# Corporate Sustainability Strategies Are Often Easier Said Than Done

Corporate sustainability strategies are frequently discussed but often face significant implementation challenges. A study conducted in the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region highlights the complexities involved in translating sustainability goals into actionable plans. The research, which surveyed 422 individuals across Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman, found that stakeholders generally perceive corporate sustainability strategies positively and are willing to invest in more effective approaches. However, the study also identifies gaps in current practices and proposes a novel model, the Reachable, Meaningful, and Fun (RMF) framework, to enhance stakeholder engagement and improve the effectiveness of corporate sustainability strategies [^1].

## Background

Sustainable development is defined as a comprehensive process that integrates social, economic, and environmental dimensions. Corporate sustainability, as a subset of sustainable development, involves the integration of these three pillars into a company's strategic planning and operational practices. This concept was first articulated by Baumgartner and Ebner (2010), who emphasized the importance of aligning corporate strategies with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The study by Abdeldayem, Aldulaimi, and Baqi (2022) builds on this foundation, examining how stakeholder engagement can be enhanced to ensure more effective implementation of corporate sustainability strategies.

## Corporate Sustainability Strategies

Corporate sustainability strategies (CSS) are designed to integrate environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations into a company's operations. These strategies aim to create long-term value for stakeholders while addressing global challenges such as climate change, resource depletion, and social inequality. However, the study by Abdeldayem et al. (2022) reveals that while there is a high level of stakeholder awareness and willingness to invest in sustainability, the actual implementation of these strategies remains challenging.

The research highlights several key challenges, including the lack of clear and consistent sustainability strategies, the difficulty in aligning short-term and long-term goals, and the limited integration of sustainability into corporate governance and decision-making processes. Additionally, the study notes that many companies do not link management incentives to sustainability performance, which can hinder the effectiveness of their sustainability initiatives.

## Stakeholder Engagement

Stakeholder engagement is a critical component of corporate sustainability strategies. The study by Abdeldayem et al. (2022) emphasizes the importance of involving stakeholders in the development and implementation of sustainability strategies. This includes not only internal stakeholders such as employees and management but also external stakeholders such as customers, communities, and regulatory bodies. The proposed RMF model aims to enhance stakeholder engagement by making sustainability initiatives more accessible, meaningful, and enjoyable for all involved.

## Challenges in Implementation

Despite the growing awareness of the importance of corporate sustainability, the implementation of these strategies remains a significant challenge. The study by Abdeldayem et al. (2022) identifies several barriers to effective implementation, including the lack of clear strategies, limited resources, and the difficulty in measuring the impact of sustainability initiatives. Additionally, the study notes that many companies do not have organized plans or roadmaps to achieve their sustainability goals, which can lead to inconsistent implementation and limited progress.

## Recommendations

To address these challenges, the study proposes the RMF model as a framework for enhancing stakeholder engagement and improving the effectiveness of corporate sustainability strategies. The model emphasizes the importance of making sustainability initiatives reachable, meaningful, and fun for all stakeholders. This approach is intended to foster greater participation and commitment from stakeholders, ultimately leading to more successful implementation of sustainability strategies.
