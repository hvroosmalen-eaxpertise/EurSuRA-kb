--- # How to Go With and Beyond the GDP in Favour of Sustainability? The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) has long been the primary measure of a country's economic performance, but its limitations in capturing sustainability and long-term well-being have led to calls for alternative indicators. This article explores the integration of the GDP with the Inclusive Wealth Index (IWI) to provide a more comprehensive assessment of economic progress and its impact on sustainability. By combining these two metrics, the article argues that states can better communicate their economic health and sustainability efforts to stakeholders. ## Background The GDP, a widely used indicator of economic activity, measures the total value of goods and services produced within a country. While it is effective for comparing economic performance across countries, it fails to account for environmental degradation, social equity, and the depletion of natural resources. These shortcomings have prompted researchers and policymakers to seek alternative metrics that reflect a broader understanding of economic and social well-being. The Inclusive Wealth Index (IWI) is one such alternative. It measures the total value of a country's produced capital, human capital, and natural capital, providing a more holistic view of a nation's wealth. By incorporating these elements, the IWI aims to reflect the sustainability of economic growth and the well-being of future generations. ## Integration of GDP and IWI The article proposes the integration of the GDP with the IWI to enhance the communication of a state's economic and sustainability performance. This approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of economic growth by considering both the flow of economic activity (as measured by GDP) and the stock of capital (as measured by IWI).  The IWI is particularly useful for reporting on sustainability and Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) factors. It includes data on natural capital, human capital, and produced capital, which are essential for assessing the long-term viability of economic growth. By incorporating these elements, the IWI provides a more comprehensive picture of a country's wealth and its potential for sustainable development. ## Results and Implications The analysis of data from six countriesâ€”France, Japan, Nigeria, Norway, Russia, and the United Kingdomâ€”reveals that the inclusion of the IWI significantly increases the reported assets of the state.
