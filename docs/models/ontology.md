# Ontology: Sustainability, Due Diligence, and Digital Competence Knowledge Base

---

- **Regulatory Instrument** (A formal rule, directive, or set of recommendations issued by a recognised authority to govern organisational behaviour)
  - **European Union Directive** (A binding legislative act of the EU requiring member states to achieve a particular result)
    - **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)** (EU directive (2023) establishing mandatory sustainability reporting obligations for large undertakings)
      - Instance examples: CSRD 2023, downstream SME information requests
  - **International Guidelines** (Government-backed or intergovernmental recommendations for responsible conduct)
    - **OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises** (Government-backed recommendations for responsible business conduct covering human rights, labour, environment, and anti-corruption; last revised 2011)
      - Instance examples: OECD Guidelines 2011, Dutch NCP implementation
    - **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs)** (UN Human Rights Council-endorsed framework articulating state duties and business responsibilities regarding human rights)
      - Instance examples: UNGPs 2011
  - **Reporting Standard** (A structured set of requirements or criteria for disclosing sustainability or competence-related information)
    - **Mandatory Reporting Standard** (A standard with legally binding application)
      - **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)** (Mandatory sustainability reporting standards adopted under CSRD, requiring full Double Materiality Assessment)
        - Instance examples: ESRS sector-agnostic standards, ESRS sector-specific standards
    - **Voluntary Reporting Standard** (A standard adopted at the discretion of the reporting organisation)
      - **Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards** (Most widely adopted voluntary sustainability reporting framework for economic, environmental, and social impact disclosure)
        - Instance examples: GRI Universal Standards, GRI Topic Standards
      - **Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standards for SMEs (VSME)** (Voluntary standards designed for small and medium-sized enterprises responding to downstream CSRD information requests)
        - Instance examples: VSME framework referenced in EFRAG webinar 2026
  - **Digital Competence Framework** (A structured reference framework describing knowledge, skills, and attitudes for digital competence)
    - **DigComp 3.0** (Fifth edition of the European Digital Competence Framework, published 2025 by the JRC, incorporating AI, cybersecurity, digital rights, and misinformation; first to include learning outcomes)
      - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 (2025), Digital Skills Indicator (DSI)

---

- **Reporting Principle** (A conceptual rule guiding what and how information must be disclosed)
  - **Materiality** (The threshold or criterion determining which sustainability topics are significant enough to report)
    - **Double Materiality** (ESRS principle requiring disclosure of both financial materiality and impact materiality)
      - **Financial Materiality** (The effect of sustainability factors on the organisation's financial position)
        - Instance examples: climate-related financial risk disclosure
      - **Impact Materiality** (The organisation's actual or potential effects on people and the environment)
        - Instance examples: human rights impacts, environmental degradation disclosures

---

- **Organisational Process** (A structured, repeatable set of activities carried out by an enterprise to fulfil a responsibility)
  - **Due Diligence Process** (Continuous process through which enterprises identify, cease, prevent, and mitigate adverse impacts on human rights, labour rights, and the environment)
    - **Six-Step Due Diligence Framework** (Operationalisation of due diligence published by the Dutch NCP, comprising six sequential and iterative steps)
      - **Step 1 – CSR Policy Integration** (Developing, endorsing, publishing, and embedding a CSR policy into management systems and supplier communications)
        - Instance examples: CSR policy publication, employee training programmes
      - **Step 2 – Identify and Assess Adverse Impacts** (Conducting CSR risk analysis mapping actual or potential adverse impacts across business activities and relationships)
        - Instance examples: supply chain risk mapping, human rights impact assessments
      - *(Steps 3–6 referenced as sequential iterative steps within the framework)*
    - **Synonymous Concepts for Due Diligence**
      - **Gepaste Zorgvuldigheid** (Dutch term: appropriate care; synonym for due diligence)
        - Instance examples: Dutch NCP guidance documents
      - **Ketenverantwoordelijkheid** (Dutch term: supply chain responsibility; synonym for due diligence)
        - Instance examples: Dutch NCP guidance documents

---

- **Assessment Activity** (A formal evaluative activity conducted by an organisation to determine scope or significance of sustainability matters)
  - **Double Materiality Assessment (DMA)** (Formal assessment required under ESRS to determine both financial and impact materiality across sustainability topics)
    - Instance examples: DMA conducted by large undertakings under CSRD

---

- **Organisational Concept** (A category describing types of organisations or organisational roles within the sustainability landscape)
  - **Reporting Organisation** (An organisation subject to or voluntarily adopting sustainability reporting obligations)
    - **Large Undertaking** (Organisation meeting EU size thresholds; subject to mandatory CSRD/ESRS obligations)
      - Instance examples: multinational enterprises, large listed companies
    - **Small and Medium-Sized Enterprise (SME)** (Organisation below large undertaking thresholds; recipient of downstream CSRD information requests; may adopt VSME voluntarily)
      - Instance examples: SMEs in supply chains of CSRD-obligated companies
    - **Multinational Enterprise (MNE)** (Enterprise operating across borders; primary addressee of OECD Guidelines and UNGP due diligence obligations)
      - Instance examples: MNEs operating in the Netherlands
  - **Governance Body** (An institution responsible for developing, overseeing, or implementing standards and guidelines)
    - **European Financial Reporting Advisory Group (EFRAG)** (EU advisory body responsible for developing ESRS and VSME standards)
      - Instance examples: EFRAG ESRS development, EFRAG VSME webinar 2026
    - **Dutch National Contact Point (NCP)** (National implementation body for OECD Guidelines in the Netherlands; provides guidance, mediation, and dispute resolution)
      - Instance examples: Dutch NCP Six-Step Due Diligence Framework publication 2021
    - **Joint Research Centre (JRC)** (European Commission research body; publisher of DigComp 3.0 in collaboration with DG EMPL)
      - Instance examples: JRC DigComp 3.0 publication 2025

- **Competence Concept** (A structured description of knowledge, skills, and attitudes required in a particular domain)
  - **Digital Competence** (Knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed to operate effectively in digital environments, as defined by DigComp 3.0)
    - **Competence Area** (A thematic grouping of related digital competences within DigComp 3.0)
      - **Information and Data Literacy** (Competences related to finding, evaluating, and managing digital information and data)
        - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Area 1
      - **Communication and Collaboration** (Competences related to interacting and collaborating through digital technologies)
        - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Area 2
      - **Digital Content Creation** (Competences related to creating and editing digital content)
        - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Area 3
      - **Safety** (Competences related to protecting devices, data, privacy, and wellbeing in digital environments; incorporates cybersecurity and digital rights)
        - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Area 4
      - **Problem-Solving** (Competences related to identifying and resolving digital problems and developing digital solutions)
        - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Area 5
  - **Proficiency Level** (A structured gradation describing degrees of digital competence from foundational to highly specialised)
    - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 proficiency levels (Foundation through Highly Specialised)
  - **Learning Outcome** (A granular, policy-aligned statement describing what a learner knows, understands, or is able to do after completing a learning process; introduced for the first time in DigComp 3.0)
    - Instance examples: DigComp 3.0 Learning Outcomes section

- **Sustainability Topic** (A subject area encompassing environmental, social, or governance dimensions relevant to reporting and due diligence)
  - **Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG)** (Standardised non-financial dimensions used to assess organisational sustainability impacts, risks, and performance)
    - **Environmental** (Dimension covering an organisation's impacts and dependencies on the natural environment)
      - Instance examples: climate change, biodiversity, pollution disclosures
    - **Social** (Dimension covering an organisation's impacts on people, including workers and communities)
      - Instance examples: human rights, labour rights, supply chain working conditions
    - **Governance** (Dimension covering organisational structures, oversight, and conduct)
      - Instance examples: anti-corruption, board oversight of sustainability
  - **International Corporate Social Responsibility (ICSR)** (Framework embedding due diligence and responsible conduct obligations within international business norms)
    - Instance examples: OECD Guidelines, UNGPs, Dutch ICSR agreements
